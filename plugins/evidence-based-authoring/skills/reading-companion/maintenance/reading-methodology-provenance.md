# Provenance of the reading methodology

One line per source reference behind a claim of `references/reading-methodology.md`, keyed by its section (§1 … §13) and the claim, so that the methodology line and its provenance line together carry everything known. Each line holds the citation as it stood in the methodology text, then the claim it backs; the evidence mark (**high**, **moderate**, **low**, **heuristic**) stays on the claim in the methodology. The grouped bibliography the methodology carried closes this file.

## By section

- **§2 Forgetting is interference** · Murre & Dros 2015 — the retention bonus after sleep (~24 h).
- **§2 The reliable signal of knowledge** · Koriat & Bjork — fluency illusion / foresight bias.
- **§2 A long-term effect without holding the fact** · Sparrow et al. 2011 — transactive / external memory.
- **§3 Most books do not deserve analytical reading** · Adler & Van Doren, *How to Read a Book* — the inspection filter; the four levels and the four analytical questions of the same section name the method.
- **§3 Formulate the goal and 3–5 questions** · Pan & Carpenter 2023 — prequestioning improves later assimilation.
- **§3 Speed reading is refuted** · Rayner et al. 2016 — the speed–accuracy trade-off; RSVP/Spritz worsen comprehension above ~250 wpm.
- **§4 Only two techniques of ten have high utility** · Dunlosky et al. 2013 (PSPI) — retrieval practice and distributed practice high; highlighting and rereading at the bottom.
- **§4.1 Read by chapters with retrieval** · Roediger & Karpicke 2006 — over 2 days rereading lost ~56% of reproducible material, testing ~13%.
- **§4.2 Feynman check** · Dunlosky et al. 2013 — self-explanation rated moderate.
- **§4 Desirable difficulty** · Bjork & Bjork — retrieval, interleaving and generation improve long-term retention at the price of visible slowing.
- **§5.1 Distribute the reviews** · Cepeda et al. 2006 — distributed reviews beat massed ones at equal cost.
- **§5.2 Let at least one sleep pass** · Diekelmann & Born 2010 — consolidation in sleep, resistance to interference.
- **§5.4 Retell it to others** · Nestojko et al. 2014 — the expectation of teaching yields fuller, better-organized recall.
- **§5.4 Retell it to others** · Fiorella & Mayer 2013 — on a delayed test the best are those who actually taught.
- **§6 Three note types** · Ahrens / Luhmann frame — practical, not controlled-verified.
- **§7 Why the line falls exactly here** · MIT EEG (Kosmyna et al. 2025, preprint); Barcaui 2025 (RCT, preliminary) — the cognitive-offloading signals.
- **§8 Implementation intentions** · Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 — self-examination by 100% of if-then planners against 53% with intention alone; meta-analysis d ≈ 0.65.
- **§9 The habit period** · Lally et al. 2010 — median ~66 days to automaticity, spread 18–254.
- **§10 The Matthew effect** · Stanovich 1986 — the reinforcing loop of background and reading.
- **§12 Far transfer is contested** · Barnett & Ceci vs Detterman — the century-long dispute.
- **§12 Retrieval practice on complex conceptual material** · van Gog & Sweller vs Karpicke & Aue — the dispute.
- **§13 Constructing and self-correcting** · Chi & Wylie 2014 — ICAP, generating outranks receiving.
- **§13 Constructing and self-correcting** · Bisra et al. 2018 — self-explanation g ≈ 0.55; self-generated prompts beat ready-made explanations.
- **§13.1 Diagnose first** · van de Pol et al. 2010 — the contingent-teaching cycle diagnose → check → intervene → check.
- **§13.3 One graduated, contingent question** · Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976 — the contingent-shift principle.
- **§13.3 One graduated, contingent question** · Chi et al. 2001 — tutors suppressed to prompts still produced learning.
- **§13.3 Default to under-helping** · van de Pol et al. 2015 — frequent help erases the benefit.
- **§13.4 Feedback elaborates** · Van der Kleij et al. 2015 — knowledge-of-results d ≈ 0.05; elaborated d ≈ 0.49.
- **§13.5 Never a quiz** · Herbel-Eisenmann & Breyfogle 2005; Mehan 1979 — funneling / the IRE triad, qualitative.
- **§13.5 Never a quiz** · Kluger & DeNisi 1996, 607 effect sizes — feedback drawing attention to the self decreased performance in 38% of cases; the same source backs the feedback-locus mechanism of §13 Honest limits.
- **§13.5 Never a quiz** · Roll et al. 2011 — graded hint ladders get gamed for answers.
- **§13.6a Know when to simply explain** · Sweller & Cooper 1985; Atkinson et al. 2003 — the worked-example effect, d ≈ 0.55.
- **§13.6b Know when to simply explain** · Sinha & Kapur 2021; Schwartz & Bransford 1998 — "a time for telling" / productive failure, observed g ≈ 0.36.
- **§13.6 Unguided struggle** · Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006 — unguided struggle without footing overloads working memory.
- **§13.7 Stop re-explaining** · Kalyuga et al. 2003 — expertise reversal.
- **§13 Honest limits** · Bloom — the "2-sigma" tutoring figure; VanLehn 2011 — realistic one-to-one gains d ≈ 0.4–0.8.
