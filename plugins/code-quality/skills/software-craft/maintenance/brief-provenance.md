# Provenance of the design brief rules

Maintainer record, read in no run. One line per rule of the design brief — the sections `SKILL.md` §Phase 3 and `plugins/code-quality/agents/software-developer.md` § Design dispatch state, the design note's §Design → Build contract records and `scripts/brief-gate.py` checks; the run set states the rule alone, and a rule and its line here change together. The cards keep their own maps (`craft-cards-provenance.md` and the language maps).

- **Decisions — two structurally different approaches per significant decision, compared before one is chosen** · Dow, S. P.; Glassco, A.; Kass, J.; Schwarz, M.; Schwartz, D. L.; Klemmer, S. R., "Parallel prototyping leads to better design results, more divergence, and increased self-efficacy", ACM Transactions on Computer-Human Interaction 17(4), article 18, 2010, doi:10.1145/1879831.1879836 — **relayed** (the publisher's copy is paywalled and the Stanford course copy answers 404; the statements are quoted from the Human Factors International newsletter summary, https://www.humanfactors.com/newsletters/parallel_prototyping.html, fetched): "In the Serial condition, participants received feedback after creating each prototype"; "In the Parallel condition, participants created three prototypes, received feedback on all three"; "Ads created in the Parallel condition significantly outperformed those from the Serial condition" "based on both click-through data and expert ratings"; "Parallel prototypes were in fact found to be more diverse as rated by experts" · formulation: Ousterhout, *A Philosophy of Software Design*, ch. 11 "Design it Twice" — the trade book names the practice and is not its evidence · notes: the experiment's designers were novices making web advertisements, and the transfer to a module design is the house inference; the rule is a step of the design dispatch, not a card, and the gate checks only that every listed decision carries its `chosen:` and `alternative:`
- **Decisions — the brief shows the operator the alternative beside the chosen approach** · Tohidi, M.; Buxton, W.; Baecker, R.; Sellen, A., "Getting the right design and the design right: testing many is better than one", Proceedings of CHI 2006, ACM, doi:10.1145/1124772.1124960 — fetched (the abstract, from the Microsoft Research publication page, https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/research/publication/getting-the-design-right-and-the-right-design-testing-many-is-better-than-one/): "when presented with a single design, users give significantly higher ratings and less negative feedback, compared to when presented with the same design in a group of three alternatives"; "by presenting users with alternative design solutions, subjective ratings will be more accurate and one will obtain more and stronger criticisms"; "usability testing in itself, even when multiple designs are presented, is not an effective vehicle for soliciting constructive suggestions about how to improve the design from end users" · notes: the study's raters were end users of an interface, and the operator confirming a brief is the house transfer; the alternative is written as the subject of the present-tense fact that blocks it, the form the repository's D-06 rule card admits in a working file; the third statement bounds the rule — the alternative sharpens the operator's criticism of the brief, it does not produce the design
- **Operations — a module or type other code calls lists its operations with inputs, output and the condition a caller must meet** · Parnas, D. L., "On the criteria to be used in decomposing systems into modules", Communications of the ACM 15(12), 1972, doi:10.1145/361598.361623 — fetched (University of South Carolina course copy, https://www.cse.sc.edu/~mgv/csce590f20/ParnasCriteria.pdf): "the interfaces are more abstract; they consist primarily in the function names and the numbers and types of the parameters"; "Every module in the second decomposition is characterized by its knowledge of a design decision which it hides from all others. Its interface or definition was chosen to reveal as little as possible about its inner workings"; Meyer, B., "Applying 'Design by Contract'", IEEE Computer 25(10), 1992, doi:10.1109/2.161279 — fetched (KTH course copy, https://www.kth.se/social/files/59526bfb56be5b4f17000807/meyer-92-contracts.pdf): "it is necessary to specify the relationship between the client (the caller) and the supplier (the called routine) as precisely as possible"; "The precondition expresses requirements that any call must satisfy if it is to be correct; the postcondition expresses properties that are ensured in return by the execution of the call" · notes: the brief keeps the failure outcomes in `## Failure paths`, so an operation line carries the caller's condition and the output, and the error it raises is one line of that section; the gate does not check an operation line, since its content is semantic
- **Gate — `scripts/brief-gate.py` checks the brief's shape before the confirmation and after a fix round amends it** · house convention, hands-on: the shape rules of the brief are mechanical — seven sections in order, a `path:Symbol` anchor, a kind and a file per step, `chosen:` and `alternative:` per decision, one line per failure path, a location per test entry — and a script checks them without an agent (the repository's rule that agents judge and scripts check); the section list is the Design → Build contract of `research/2026-09-26_software-craft-cards.md`, and `maintenance/tests/test_brief_gate.py` holds one case per defect
