# Question Map: The scientific method as an operational procedure for accepting statements, in the formulation this repository adopts

## Overview
The nine method agents generated 345 questions. The coverage audit added 22 more to fill gaps and flagged 151 duplicate pairs among the 345, far above the usual density. After those pairs, and the further near-duplicates in the same chains, were merged into their stronger formulations, this report holds 214 questions in 12 clusters, a ratio of about 1.6 to 1 over the 345, or 1.7 to 1 counting the 22 fill-ins. All nine agents completed, so coverage is not partial. Three clusters stand out as unexpected. "The method applied to itself" was raised independently by six methods: is the sentence about the shared core a claim, could the method's own record be tested, what does running it cost. "Singular, diagnostic and applied tasks beyond skill rules" was raised by six methods. Each one arrived at the same gap: a repeatable, pre-recorded test does not fit a one-off outage, a single unit or an inference to the best explanation. "Independence, authority and whose knowledge counts" was raised by seven methods. Most of them asked what the independent reader is worth when a model writes the hypothesis, generates the cases, runs the test and judges it. The coverage audit found five under-covered dimensions: stakeholder breadth, cross-domain breadth, completeness of the source slots, the bedrock of the assumptions, and where and when each step happens. Its fill-in questions closed the first, fourth and fifth. Cross-domain breadth and the source slots (primary passages of the followed texts, non-Anglophone methodology, practitioner communities) remain partly open and appear again under Open Terrain.

## Questions by Dimension

### Philosophical lineage and fidelity to the cited texts
1. Which lines of the philosophy of science does the sentence "they hold what the main lines of the philosophy of science share" cover — Popper's falsificationism, Lakatos's research programmes, Kuhn, Bayesian confirmation, Mayo's error statistics, Mill's methods, the new experimentalism — and why does method.md name none of them, nor what each contributed and what was dropped as not shared?
2. Which main line rejects at least one of the seven rules — Bayesian confirmation, Lakatos, Kuhn, Mayo, Cartwright on causal claims, decision-theoretic accounts of evidence — and is there in fact a shared core across them, or is the formulation silently choosing one line and calling it the overlap?
3. Historians and philosophers from Feyerabend to Chalmers argued there is no single scientific method; under what reading is "the scientific method" the wrong name for seven house rules, better described as quality assurance, audit or evidence-based practice, and what would change if it were renamed?
4. Before the repository framed rule admission as "the scientific method judges a claim by its test", which earlier framings did its history hold — the authority of a cited source, the prestige of a venue, the maintainer's experience — and what did each let in or keep out that the current framing does not?
5. Why is the topic framed as a procedure for accepting statements rather than for discovering, explaining or deciding, and what do the philosophers who abandoned the split between the context of discovery and the context of justification say such a framing leaves out?
6. When the method's own description was rewritten to stop defining itself by contrast with citing a source, which questions about testimony and trust in others' reports left the frame together with the contrasted rule?
7. Does the method, the README's list of texts or the provenance records say, for each rule, which text it takes the rule from — Mill's account of hypothesis (1843, 7th ed. 1868), Peirce's self-correcting inquiry, Popper (1934), Lakatos (1970), Mayo (1996, 2018) — in which edition and on which page, and where it departs from the source, applying to itself the requirement that a norm be opened "with its issuer and version"?
8. How should the question whether the cited texts say what the method takes from them be checked — by opening each passage and quoting it beside the rule it supports, read by someone other than the method's author — and is rule 4's own check (open the source, find the test in it) applied to the method's own sources?
9. What would a historian of ideas accept as evidence that a rule was taken from a text rather than attached to it by a later citation, and what does each citation have to quote to show that?
10. What does the passage of Mill's System of Logic cited for the definition of a hypothesis say in its context, Book III ch. XIV, and does Mill's treatment of hypotheses in the same chapter admit anything the method would class as opinion?
11. Which primary passages of Duhem's Aim and Structure of Physical Theory and of Quine's "Two Dogmas of Empiricism" state the holism of testing, as distinct from reaching them only through Gelman and Shalizi, and do they bear on a claim tested by a run of a command or only on theoretical physics?
12. Which texts of empirical software engineering — the tradition of controlled experiments with developers and of threats-to-validity checklists — set standards for comparing a technique with and without it, and what would they add to rule 3?
13. Which texts in the provenance records were opened once and are now quoted without re-reading, what would show whether each still says what the record takes from it, and what feature of the layout — the method file, the README's list of texts and the provenance files kept apart — lets the link between a rule and its source drift with no check failing?
14. Should the record format be checked against the TOP 2025 transparency and openness guidelines as a norm, and does the method present that norm as a norm under its own rule 1?
15. Which once-standard procedures for accepting statements were tried and abandoned — refutation by a single counter-instance, the verifiability criterion of the logical positivists, Carnap's inductive logic, the null ritual of significance testing — and which of their documented failures do the table of standings and rule 7 actually guard against?
16. Is the separation of decision from claim bedrock, or does it derive from the contested fact–value dichotomy (Hume's is–ought gap, Putnam's argument that the dichotomy collapses), and does it survive the values-in-science literature — Douglas on inductive risk, Longino's social epistemology, methodological pluralism — which holds that the choice of a threshold is itself value-laden?
17. Popper held that "basic statements are accepted as the result of a decision or agreement; and to that extent they are conventions", and Rodych argues this conventionalism undermines Popper's own answer to induction; does the method take falsificationism without the conventionalist empirical basis it depends on, and does it say so?
18. If the cited sources were read for what they reject — Popper against induction, Mill's methods against hypothesis-first inquiry, Kuhn against rule-based acceptance — would the formulation still say it follows them?
19. Which positions that would destabilise the scheme are absent from the texts it follows — Bayesian epistemology, Mayo's severity as a quantity, Lakatos on progressive versus degenerating programmes, Kuhn and Feyerabend on theory choice, Pearl's causal inference, measurement theory — and was their absence recorded as a decision?
20. Does the shared-core claim survive traditions outside Anglo-American philosophy — German Wissenschaftstheorie, the Soviet and Russian methodology of science, French historical epistemology — where "закон" and "Gesetz" name a necessary connection rather than a well-tested regularity, and which of their available texts defines "law" or "hypothesis" differently enough to change a standing?
21. How did metascience after the replication crisis — preregistration, registered reports, multiverse and many-analysts studies — redefine what "a test" is, which of those redefinitions did the formulation take in under its own names, and what does the record up to November 2026 show about how often pre-registration, a control comparison and repeated runs actually change the verdict on a claim?
22. Which criticisms of the replication-reform agenda (Metascience 2025's "Does metascience need to change?", SIPS 2025's "What went wrong?") has the method inherited without examining, and which failure modes of the replication crisis — low power, flexible analysis, publication bias, measurement error — would pass every check question unnoticed, given that preregistration and replication, each compatible with the seven rules, did not by themselves prevent low replication rates?
23. For each of the seven rules, which formulation is the reform movement's actual position and which is a stricter version no source defends, as Wagenmakers charges that Devezer et al. demolish straw men no reformer holds?

### Kinds of statement and the logical form of claims
1. Why does "exit status zero means success" need no test while "the agent reads the file before editing more often" does — what makes the first a norm and not a claim?
2. If the result of a run is a fact but a conclusion drawn from it is a claim, where exactly does the fact end and the claim begin when the result is written down?
3. Could the four kinds of statement alone, without the test machinery, already improve how design documents, code reviews and post-mortems separate a taste from a finding?
4. If two independent readers sort the same hundred statements into the four kinds, how often do they agree, and what would low agreement do to every standing that depends on the sort?
5. When do the four kinds merge — a definition that hides an empirical claim (an operational definition of "defect"), a norm whose issuer claims it works, a threshold decision that is really a claim about the cost of error, thick terms such as "calm" or "readable" — and does rule 1 then misclassify the statement and shield a claim from its test?
6. Law separates questions of fact, questions of law and mixed questions; does the four-kind table give a way to split a statement like "this technique meets the goal" into its decision and its claim, or does it force a single kind on it, so that the acceptance path needs a fifth branch for mixed statements?
7. What is left out by the four kinds — mathematical and logical truths, descriptive and estimation claims ("the median run costs 40k tokens"), predictions about the future, counterfactuals, statements about the meaning of a text?
8. Does a proof, a type check, an exhaustive enumeration or a simulation count as a test, which kind of statement is "this design cannot deadlock", and is "a logical inference is worth what its premises are worth" enough when an invalid inference can fail even with true premises?
9. How does a universal claim ("the agent always reads the file first") differ from a statistical one ("reads it more often") in what a single failure refutes, and does the table of standings distinguish them?
10. Is the refutability rule, phrased as "the outcome that would refute it", tight enough for claims that are only ever statistical, such as agent behaviour rates, given Gelman and Shalizi's judgement that Popper's deductive notion of a test is "plainly inadequate for statistical hypotheses"?
11. An existential claim ("some prompt makes the model leak the secret") is proved by one success and refuted by no number of failures, and probabilistic, holistic and mechanistic claims also resist a single refuting observation; when does strict refutability become over-strict and exclude statements the philosophy of science treats as legitimate?
12. Does the definition of a fact as "an observation anyone can repeat or open" hide a convention that rule 1 says must be presented as a decision, and if what counts as a fact depends on the instrument and its theory (an LLM judge, a regex scorer, a rubric), at which point does a fact become a claim that needs its own test?
13. Does the "definition" kind, resting on agreement alone, let an unvalidated construct pass into a measured claim, as the review of 445 LLM benchmarks resting on "unclear definitions or weak analytical methods" suggests it can?
14. If the kind "norm" were removed and every accepted standard treated as a claim awaiting test, what would break in practice, and what does the kind silently exempt from testing?
15. How does the formulation record a norm whose underlying claim has been refuted, what prevents a practice a regulation mandates from being dropped because a local test found it does not work better, and which of the two opposite entries governs action?
16. Does a prescription really decompose into a goal (the owner's decision) and a means–end claim, what happens to rules whose goal is discovered or revised only by testing the means, and must an owner ever defend the goal itself, or can any goal license a rule?
17. When two tests of a rule weigh different values — accuracy, cost, reader time — which of the four kinds holds the weighing, given that even Chang's pluralism, on Kusch's critique, gives no overall verdict on which bundle of epistemic values to prefer?
18. When appraising someone else's research, is "this paper's test was severe" a claim, a check against a norm, or a fifth kind of statement the formulation lacks?
19. What development would make the four kinds obsolete or insufficient — norms turned into machine-checked specifications, decisions inferred from preference data — and what observation would show the taxonomy no longer sorts the statements met in practice?

### Auxiliaries, refutation and revision
1. Why does one passing test move a hypothesis to supported while one failing test only leaves it waiting for a repetition, when both come from the same test with the same chance of error, and is that asymmetry intended?
2. Would a maintainer feel relief or dread at the rule that a single failure only "waits for its repetition", and does that feeling predict whether failed runs get recorded at all?
3. What harm follows when a rule whose failure is costly stays in force while the second failure is awaited, or when nobody ever retests a claim, so that it is never refuted?
4. What assumption about the cost of a wrongly admitted rule against a wrongly withdrawn one makes the asymmetry look reasonable, why do the standings carry no stated error rate, prior or decision threshold that would expose it, and should that assumption be recorded as the owner's decision instead of being built into the method?
5. What replaces a fixed count of two failures as the threshold for "refuted" when the samples of the two tests differ in size and variance?
6. If every failed test indicts a bundle — the claim, the prompt, the measure, the harness, the model version, the grader — what does "refuted" pick out, why is the claim the part that gets withdrawn, and what must change in the step from "failed repeatably" to "refuted" so that the auxiliaries are named?
7. Does the acceptance path have a branch for "the comparison or the measure was mis-specified", as Mayo-Wilson's reading of severe testing calls for rejecting the underlying modelling assumption when data fit every considered hypothesis poorly, and how is an honest redesign after a test told from fitting?
8. How does a ladder ending in "refuted" accommodate claims everyone already knows to be literally false but useful within bounds — Gelman and Shalizi's "complex models that we know ahead of time to be false", improved by continuous expansion rather than selection?
9. Is rule 7's "a second failure under the same conditions moves the standing to refuted" naive falsificationism by Lakatos's definition, which rejects the mono-theoretical model of criticism and holds that theories are born into "an ocean of anomalies"?
10. When a published test and a repository test disagree and neither names a separating condition, but they used different populations so that "the same bounds" were never met, does the claim become contested at all?
11. What obliges anyone to design the test that would resolve a contested standing, what happens meanwhile to the rules that depend on it, and should rival rules stay in force side by side, as Chang's active normative pluralism suggests, rather than only in the list of hypotheses?
12. When a claim is refuted, or downgraded from law to supported, how does the change reach every record, rule, skill and decision that listed it as a premise, as an audit restatement discloses what relied on the misstated figure?
13. Does rule 7 weigh what it costs to withdraw a rule that other skills, agents and records rely on, as stare decisis overrules a precedent only with special justification?
14. Does the revision rule correct errors in practice, when refutation needs a repeated failure under the same bounds and those bounds include a model version that keeps changing?
15. Does the record of failed runs and refuted claims, which "stays", grow into a body of negative results that anyone reads or reuses, or does it only accumulate?
16. Does the per-claim refutability rule miss defects that show only at the level of a whole series of rules and their protective auxiliaries — Lakatos's programme that is "falsifiable but unscientific" — and at what number of claims tested by one maintainer with the same harness and judge does the work become a research programme whose hard core is never itself put to a test?

### The ladder of standings: grades, degrees and stakes
1. When a report produced with this method calls a claim "supported", how does its reader know how much weaker that is than "law" without reading the method?
2. What distinguishes "law" from "supported" — how many independent tests, how varied the conditions, who judges "varied" and "independent" — and is that judgement a claim or a decision under rule 1?
3. When the conditions that varied were seeds and phrasings while model family, task kind and reader never varied, or the "independent" tests share data, implementers, models or graders — two copies of one witness, as a historian counts them — what keeps such tests from earning "law"?
4. Is "law" reachable at all for claims about prompt and agent techniques whose test conditions never recur, and if the distinction from "supported" were removed, which rule or decision in the repository would change?
5. What is the formulation's measure of support — a binary pass, an effect size with an interval, a posterior probability, a severity in Mayo's sense — and does the choice change any standing?
6. Which side of the statistics wars does "supported: it passed a test that could have refuted it" commit to, and would a Bayesian reading of the same runs (a Bayes factor, a posterior) give the same record a different standing?
7. Is falsification the bedrock of acceptance here or derivable from error statistics or Bayesian confirmation, and which of the seven rules would survive if a Bayesian reading of "supported" were substituted, given that Gelman himself calls his falsificationist Bayesianism underdeveloped?
8. What would the acceptance path look like if a standing were a degree of belief or corroboration updated by each test — regions on a continuous scale — and does a skill nonetheless need discrete standings because a rule is either in force or not?
9. Under what conditions would the six standings be subsumed as a coarse special case of Bayesian credence or the value of information, and what in the formulation would remain distinctive after that?
10. When does passing one test that could have refuted the claim leave it more likely false than true — a low prior, a small effect, a small sample — so that a surprising claim and a mundane claim passing the same small test deserve different standings?
11. When does judging every origin as equal stop being sound, because a source's track record, prior plausibility or conflict of interest carries information the test alone cannot carry at a sample size anyone can afford, and should each record carry the claim's prior plausibility beside its test?
12. Could a claim enter on a cheaper kind of test — a simulation, an adversarial review, a prediction checked later in use — under a standing of its own between hypothesis and supported?
13. Does "hypothesis" cover two different states — a claim waiting for a test and a claim no affordable test can reach — and would a standing like an auditor's disclaimer of opinion, "accepted because testing it was unaffordable", be more honest about the second?
14. Law, medicine and auditing all scale the bar for acceptance to the stakes; is one yes-or-no "supported" gate too strict for cheap, reversible rules and too lax for costly ones, and who other than the rule's author should fix the standard, as the judge rather than a party fixes the standard of proof?

### Severity, measurement and statistical inference
1. For rule 3's own good example, "nine of ten runs with the paragraph and four of ten without", which statistical test, error rate and minimum effect decide that this tells the effect from chance, where does the formulation name them, and does it give a practitioner anything as concrete as the evals practitioners' 100–200 labelled examples per failure mode with separate dev and test splits?
2. How is "the same work done without the technique" constructed when the technique changes the work itself — a paragraph that changes which files an agent opens — so that no clean control exists, and which counterfactual is the comparison against?
3. Who chooses the comparison arm, and what makes it the strongest reasonable alternative a practitioner would otherwise use, rather than an empty prompt or a broken baseline?
4. Does rule 3's check question commit to Mayo's S-2 — that with high probability the hypothesis would not have passed so well were it false, a property of the data actually obtained — or only to the pre-test design of a comparison, and which sense of severity, Popper's or Mayo's, does the rule take?
5. Where does the record name and check the gap between the statistical hypothesis ("nine of ten runs passed") and the substantive one ("the paragraph makes the agent read the file first"), which Mayo warns a test can pass while leaving the second unprobed?
6. The rules name a comparison, repetitions and a refuting outcome but say nothing about whether the measure measures what the claim is about; if construct validity fails — a judge score that tracks length instead of quality, judges with invariance 0.945 but sensitivity 0.319, surface-only predictors reproducing 55–67% of labels — would any of the seven check questions catch it?
7. When a claim is measured on a proxy (a tool call counted as "reads the file") rather than the outcome its goal names (fewer bad edits), should the link from proxy to outcome be a tested claim of its own, as accelerated approval on a surrogate endpoint requires a confirmatory trial on the real one?
8. Where does the smallest effect worth acting on — the auditor's materiality — enter, as distinct from the direction of the effect, so that a result statistically significant but below the minimum that matters for the goal does not count as support?
9. Is "an equal or higher rate refutes it", the method's own example of a refuting outcome, a sharp null that a small test cannot refute when it is subtly false, and which questions about falsifying point nulls and "accepting" a null would its refuting outcomes face from statisticians?
10. Does forcing every claim to name a falsifying threshold without a theoretical basis produce arbitrary thresholds, as Scheel et al. warn, and which inputs a hypothesis test needs — concept formation, measurement, relations between concepts, boundary conditions, auxiliary assumptions — does the path require before a claim may become a hypothesis?
11. How does the formulation handle effects that are real but small, heterogeneous across tasks, or visible only in a distribution rather than a mean?
12. Where does the method measure an agent's adherence to a rule separately from the rule's effect, so that a rule that "fails" because it was ignored is told from one that fails when obeyed?
13. Should the severity of a test be checked by planting a claim known to be false and seeing that the test refutes it, as mutation testing judges a suite by the faults it catches, or by A/A tests and flaky-test detection?
14. Does the test of one claim against "the same work done without the technique" ever weigh the evidence against several rival claims at once, as intelligence analysts weigh evidence by how well it discriminates between competing hypotheses rather than how well it fits one?
15. Does a fact need a stated uncertainty — repeatability, reproducibility and an uncertainty budget for each number in a record, as metrology requires of a calibrated instrument?
16. Why does rule 3 rank an intervening experiment above an observation of cases as they came and say nothing of mechanism, process tracing, natural experiments or expert judgement; is the ranking taken from Mill's method of difference, medicine's evidence hierarchies or causal-inference work, and does it class legitimate causal evidence as weaker than it is, as Cartwright and the critics of evidence hierarchies argue?
17. When the only available test of a claim is observational data published by someone else, how does the intervention ranking grade it against a small intervention run in the repository?
18. Can a qualitative study — interviews, think-aloud protocols, a reading of transcripts — ever move a claim to supported, or does the formulation implicitly require counts?
19. When many hypotheses are scored on the same runs — twelve hypotheses on twelve generated cases, thresholds crossed by chance — how many false "supported" standings should the whole repository expect, and does "no fitting" or any other rule control the multiplicity and the false discovery rate across tests?
20. Which rule counts the variants of a paragraph tried before the one that was tested, as quantitative finance corrects a backtest for the number of strategies tried on the same history, and is that count recorded?
21. Does a stopping rule fixed in advance satisfy "the threshold is recorded before the test", and should rule 3 admit sequential designs with strict Type-I error control, such as the agentic sequential falsification of Huang et al., that decide when to stop as the evidence arrives?

### Pre-recording, exploration and fitting
1. When does recording "the prediction, the measure and the threshold before the test" count as done — a commit before the run, a timestamped registry, a third party — and does the method require any of them, or only say "recorded before"?
2. Who can verify that no outcome was switched when the record lives in a repository its author can rewrite, how can a report reader check that the failed runs never shown to them were recorded, and under which audit standard, with what retention and tamper evidence, could a third party confirm that the record "holds every run, the failed ones included"?
3. How does a record that holds every run tell an aborted run — stopped halfway at a rate limit — from a run in which the claim failed, and which standing did the half-run move?
4. A rule admitted on "nine of ten runs with the paragraph, four of ten without" failed in use and its rerun could not reproduce the numbers; which conditions must the record carry — model version, sampling settings, harness, surrounding context, case set — for a rerun to be possible?
5. Where do the records live relative to the rules they ground — the same file, a separate registry, a commit message — and what happens to a rule's standing when its record is moved, renamed or lost?
6. What must a pre-test record contain to be informative, and who decides, given Data Colada's view that p-hacking is inevitable whenever the analysis is ambiguous and not fully planned, and the 2026 critiques that preregistration is "cheap talk" when deviations are allowed and nobody checks the analysis against the plan?
7. What would the method gain by combining registered reports and a fixed analysis plan with its rule of no fitting, and what does it now leave to the author's honesty?
8. Does rule 6 inherit the exploratory–confirmatory split of de Groot, Greenwald and Meehl itself, or only its later preregistration form?
9. Where does exploratory work — descriptive observation, pilots, parameter-range exploration, the open-ended error analysis of some hundred traces that agent evaluation starts with — get a record and a standing, given that the standings begin at opinion and Scheel et al. call the confirmatory–exploratory split a false dichotomy?
10. Which tasks — debugging, product analytics, iterative prompt tuning, design — have exploratory, sequential or adaptive designs where pre-recording is no longer the right default, and what replaces it there without opening the door to fitting, such as an explicit exploratory phase whose results count only as origins?
11. Does the line "the observation a hypothesis grew from is its origin, and a later observation is its test" give a criterion that tells an illicitly dredged hypothesis from one formulated after the data yet supported by them, or does it discard every post-hoc discovery, and what would the path look like if it started from observations rather than a stated claim?
12. Is rule 6 over-strict in treating "recorded before the result was seen" as the only safeguard, when Devezer et al. argue that using data more than once is valid under proper conditioning, and does the method rest on the theory of exploration and the theory of reproducibility they say are still missing?
13. Does rule 6 carry weight the method never tests, if preregistration is redundant when good theories are tested, as Szollosi et al. argue, and where does the method say how a hypothesis worth testing is built in the first place?
14. Does the clause that a saving condition enters only as a new hypothesis "that predicts something new" amount to Lakatos's novelty criterion for a progressive programme, and does the method credit Lakatos for it?
15. Does "the record holds every run" apply to other people's published tests, which the method accepts once opened, or only to its own, given Data Colada's argument that the file drawer is unfixable and that disclosure of sample-size rules, measures, conditions and exclusions must suffice?
16. Under what conditions would automated research agents running thousands of tests a day make a human-readable pre-test record unenforceable, and what would replace it — a correction for multiplicity across the whole run log, a locked analysis script?

### Reproducibility and someone else's evidence
1. What counts as having "opened the source and found the test" when the paper is paywalled, withholds data, code, prompts or model versions, or reports only aggregates — does the claim fall to opinion, to hypothesis or to a weaker support — and how much of the published literature could the method then admit at all?
2. Given replication-crisis base rates, undisclosed forking paths, fabricated data and model-written papers in 2026, when does reading a results section reproduce nothing, so that "opened" stands in for "reproduced" and a reader who cannot check selective reporting, the data or the code still admits the claim as supported?
3. Why does the method put all the evidential weight in the visible design of a test and none in the process that checked it — peer review, sample size, who ran it, replication record, retraction status — what does "a test made in this repository counts the same as a published one" lose by that, and whose work does that equality make cheaper?
4. How would the formulation catch a fabricated or selectively reported result whose comparison, measure, sample and number are all in plain view, when a historian first asks whether a source is authentic and what its author stood to gain?
5. When the source was opened and the right number read, what checks that comparison, measure and sample were read together as the source meant them — relative risk not read as absolute, a subgroup not read as the whole sample, adjusted not read as unadjusted — and that the reader saw the threats to validity and the confidence interval, not only the number?
6. What does appraising someone else's research require that "the comparison, the measure, the sample and the result are in view" leaves out — risk of bias, indirectness, imprecision, inconsistency, conflicts of interest, heterogeneity, selective reporting — what can be adapted from GRADE and the Cochrane tools, and where would those tools mislead for tests of AI agents?
7. Would rule 4 admit a published test whose error rate is unknown, or one applied outside the conditions it was validated for, which a judge screening expert evidence would refuse?
8. When the method accepts someone else's test, what must the reader check about the measure's agreement with other measures, given that new benchmarks are rarely checked against established ones (Perlitz et al.)?
9. Does accepting an opened result give any weight to whether it was ever replicated, which kind of repetition — direct (about 0.2% of top-journal articles) or conceptual — does rule 4 demand and rule 7 count toward "refuted", and what share of the opened tests in the Open Science Collaboration and Many Labs projects would have passed rule 4 and still failed to replicate?
10. If the 2026 proposals to mandate the reporting of null results succeed, would a correction for publication bias become a condition of accepting an opened external test?
11. Is rule 4 a demarcation criterion, and what does it accept when a rerun of a test of one's own confirms a false claim, given Devezer et al.'s result that true results need not reproduce and false ones may?
12. If the rule that a test of one's own must "run by a command" were removed, which kinds of evidence would become admissible, and what failures does that rule silently prevent?
13. Would a claim "supported" in one repository ever reach another as more than a transfer, and what shared registry, benchmark or record format would make opening another's test practical for prompt techniques?

### Bounds of transfer and a moving system under test
1. When a rule is supported within bounds written in prose — who, what, which version — how does an agent following it tell whether the task in front of it lies inside those bounds, and which conditions are mandatory fields of the record rather than left to the author?
2. When the relevant conditions of an LLM run include the system prompt, tool set, temperature, context length and the order of examples, which differences make a new application "beyond the bounds", and does rule 5 need a procedure like a court's for showing that a new case differs in a material condition?
3. How small an edit to a supported rule — a synonym, a reordering, a translation, a neighbouring paragraph added — takes it outside the bounds of the test that supported it, and who judges that?
4. A rule is words a model reads; when the same words, read by another model or in another context, produce another behaviour, is the object a test supports the text, the text and its reader together, or the behaviour, and is a claim about a skill rule a claim about a method (the paragraph) or about a model (one version's behaviour)?
5. Must the bounds name the prompts, tasks and repositories sampled as well as the model version — does a rule tested on twelve generated documents hold beyond those twelve — and does the record state the sampling risk and the population of real documents the cases stand for, as Yarkoni's generalizability critique and audit sampling both demand?
6. Does rule 2 ensure that the verbal claim and its measured test refer to the same population of tasks, files and prompts, and how would the method catch the gap between a test of one prompt pair on one task family and a rule built on it that says "always"?
7. Should an agent act differently on a rule marked as a transfer than on one tested under its own conditions, and how should the method tell a near transfer from a far one, so that the mark still tells the reader something when nearly every applied rule is a transfer?
8. Does a transfer carry a named owner who answers when it fails, as an off-label prescriber does; will the record say that the transfer failed rather than the source's finding; and what disclosure must a transferred rule carry before it may stand in the technical documentation of a high-risk AI system?
9. How narrow can the bounds become — one repository, one model, one harness — before every supported claim is a local observation and nothing is general enough to be a rule, and can a claim be narrow when tested and widened later by repeated tests without the widening becoming fitting?
10. Does rule 5 count the move from a curated evaluation set to real sessions as a transfer, as clinicians separate efficacy from effectiveness, and can a test run under recognisable test conditions transfer to ordinary use at all, given that evaluation awareness measurably changes model behaviour?
11. A rule supported alone is assumed to keep its effect among dozens of others in the same context; when rules interact — one paragraph crowds out another, order effects, ceiling effects — are single-rule tests ever within the bounds of how rules are applied, and what tests the skill that combines them?
12. When the model, the harness or the prompt changes, when does a supported rule expire, how long does it take to notice, who notices, and what triggers a retest, so that rules bound to a model version no longer served do not stay in a skill for years?
13. If model versions are retired within months, or the time between a test and the next release shrinks to days, must every standing carry a version and an expiry, and what re-test cadence or carry-over test would the formulation then need?
14. Should a rule periodically re-earn its standing by a fresh test — as a regression test reruns on every change and a flaky test is quarantined with an exit criterion — and would a scheduled expiry tied to the version in its bounds change the standings more than any change to the seven rules would?
15. When the model under test is nondeterministic and its provider updates it silently, what does "a rerun confirms" mean, is a sampled run scored by a model judge a repeatable observation at all, does a silent update count as leaving the bounds, and is the record of the original run then the only fact left?
16. If uniformity across runs, model versions and repositories is false for a system whose weights change without notice, is any standing above hypothesis attainable for claims about agent behaviour?
17. Once a rule is supported, who watches it in use for side effects on other tasks, as post-market surveillance finds harms trials were too small to show, and can uncontrolled reports move a standing when rule 3 ranks observation below intervention?
18. When a rule in force shapes the behaviour that produces the observations used to test it, can the loop confirm itself, and at what point does the object of a claim stop being a fixed world and become a system that adapts to the test — a model trained on evaluations, users who learn a product, a metric that becomes a target?
19. Which practitioner communities beyond the evals FAQ discuss accepting or rejecting prompt and agent techniques on evidence, and which of their reports describe a technique that passed its test and then failed to transfer?

### Acting before the evidence: decisions, costs and uncertainty
1. With one idea and no time to test it, what, concretely, may one write in a skill today?
2. Who decides, before the test, how many repetitions suffice "by the cost of an error", is that cost recorded as a number a reader can check, and would a different owner reach a different standing from the same data?
3. Whose costs enter that threshold — the maintainer's, the skill's users', the people who never read the skill but bear what an agent does under it — and who represents them when the threshold is set?
4. Who owns a decision when a repository has several maintainers, a client or a team that disagrees on the goal, and how is a conflict between two owners' decisions recorded?
5. An architecture is chosen once, under a deadline, with no control group; is that choice a decision, a hypothesis that waits, or an opinion that should stay out of the design document?
6. Can "decision" become a permanent entrance for untested claims, since a rule needed before its test is in force as a decision and no term obliges its hypothesis to be tested, and which deadline, review, sunset clause or confirmatory-trial trigger, of the kinds medicine and adaptive management use, would push a waiting hypothesis to a test or take its rule out of force?
7. When the list of hypotheses grows to hundreds of waiting entries with no tests scheduled, which hypothesis is tested first, and what keeps the waiting list from becoming permanent where a test costs more than the error it would prevent?
8. What limits how much of a skill may rest on decisions, who may challenge how a statement was classified, does the burden then fall on the challenger to bring a refuting test or on the owner to bring a supporting one, and what happens to a decision whose hidden claim premise is later refuted?
9. Which questions — whether a recorded "taste" was a claim in disguise that a test would have failed — are kept off the table by the rule that a decision enters "under its owner's name"?
10. Does a rule that entered as a decision before its test become harder to test the longer it works well enough, because nobody wants to run the arm without it?
11. When a severe test of one paragraph costs a day of runs, when may the owner say that the cost of the test exceeds the cost of being wrong, and where is that judgement recorded?
12. Why does every successful branch of the acceptance path end in "enters as a rule", when debugging, design and product experiments aim at an action or a belief, and where does the method separate the standing of a claim from the decision to act on it — with costs, reversibility and the price of being wrong — so that a product team does not read "supported" as "ship it"?
13. Why does the method give no procedure for choosing while the claim a decision depends on is still a hypothesis, and where must expected value, regret analysis or the value of information take over from the acceptance path, for a team that must act now as for public decisions such as precaution or emergency policy?
14. Is admission to a rule list bedrock, or derivable from the more basic goal of acting well under uncertainty, and does the formulation need a standing or a field for "acted on under uncertainty, with the expected cost of error", which neither "decision" nor "hypothesis" captures?
15. Where waiting is itself costly — an incident, a release deadline, a risk of irreversible harm — does the formulation weigh the cost of delay against the cost of a wrong rule, or leave all of it to the owner's decision, and is "in force as a decision" the only branch the path offers for acting before evidence exists?
16. Could the owner's word as the ground of a decision be replaced by an explicit decision rule — expected utility, a threshold of acceptable loss — and would that move part of each decision into the territory of claims?
17. GRADE keeps the certainty of the evidence apart from the strength of a recommendation, so that a strong recommendation can rest on low-certainty evidence when values and costs call for it; does the formulation need a second axis for how firmly a rule is prescribed, apart from its standing?
18. Can a rule be adopted on purpose to test it, as active adaptive management chooses some actions for what they will teach, with the decision saying openly what is traded for the information?

### Singular, diagnostic and applied tasks beyond skill rules
1. An on-call engineer tests five guesses about an outage's cause in ten minutes; must each enter with a refuting outcome and a record, or is debugging outside this procedure altogether?
2. When a fix makes the symptom go away once, and the system was never run without the fix under the same load, which standing does "the bug was caused by X" reach, and is the acceptance path the right procedure for singular causal claims at all?
3. Applied in full to a production outage, the procedure makes "the fault is in module X" wait for a pre-registered threshold and repeated runs while the system stays down; which of the seven rules carry over to diagnosis of one-off events, which demands of rule 3 are over-strict for one engineer and one defect, and which procedure — differential diagnosis, root-cause analysis, bisection, a minimal reproducer, value-of-information analysis — must be named instead?
4. For a decision an agent must take within one session — choosing a fix, picking a tool now — which minimal part of the acceptance path can run in minutes, and which rule breaks first under that time budget?
5. Does "the observation a hypothesis grew from is its origin" hold when the only data is one incident or one log that cannot be produced again, and does the requirement of a repeatable test shut out claims about one-off events that historians ground well by source criticism?
6. Is there a place in the acceptance path for a claim accepted because no rival explanation survives — inference to the best explanation, the evidential paradigm of the historian, the detective and the diagnosing physician — rather than because it passed a prospective test?
7. When a rule applies to a single repository or one owner's workflow with no parallel copy, which design does rule 3 accept: a before-after-control-impact design as field ecologists use, an n-of-1 trial as clinicians use?
8. Across history, n-of-1 trials, ecology's unreplicable sites, cosmology and climate projection, is a repeatable, intervening test the right universal requirement, or does the formulation need a second acceptance path for singular and non-interventional claims, such as a debugging diagnosis or an appraisal of one study, instead of leaving them in the list of hypotheses for good?
9. In product experiments on live users, where a test cannot be rerun under the same conditions, how is reproducibility met by "opening the description" rather than repeating it?
10. Which practices of mature experimentation platforms — guardrail metrics, sequential testing with peeking control, sample-ratio-mismatch checks, novelty effects, long-term holdouts — have no counterpart among the seven rules, and under which rule, if any, would each fall?
11. If one run of a held-out suite is enough to ship a prompt on a benchmark-driven evaluation platform, what does a with-and-without comparison buy that pays for doubling the cost?
12. Applied to benchmark evaluation of prompts and agents, how would the method treat benchmark contamination and leaderboard overfitting under its rules of no fitting and bounds of transfer?
13. Used as a gate for admitting a claim into an architecture decision record, which of the seven rules would an engineering team reject as too costly?
14. Would the acceptance path gain from a safety assurance case — a structured argument from claims to evidence with explicit defeaters and the evidence that rebuts each — and how does "acceptably safe" differ from "supported"?
15. Could the predicted outcomes in the list of hypotheses be scored by calibration over many predictions, as forecasting tournaments use Brier scores, and would an author's track record, human or model, count as evidence about that author's next hypothesis?
16. Does one domain-free method fit debugging, product experiments and agent evaluation equally, or does each need its own input checks, since judging the inputs of a test "will depend on the specifics of a research area" — and is the method's scope a claim about where it works, which could be tested, or a decision about where it is applied, and does method.md say which?

When an LLM judge scores the outcomes of a test, is the judge's reliability a separate hypothesis that must be supported first, how deep does that regress go, and why is asking whether a model judge's verdict is "an observation anyone can repeat" treated as premature when the repository's own tests rest on model readers whose outputs vary between runs?

Whose knowledge — practitioners' tacit craft passed by apprenticeship, design judgement, qualitative user research, clinical-style case reasoning — is placed under "opinion", who speaks for it in the repository, and is there evidence that excluding it produces better rules, or is the exclusion itself an untested claim?

## Frontier Questions
> Ten questions whose answers would most change understanding of the topic.

1. Which main line of the philosophy of science rejects at least one of the seven rules, and is there in fact a shared core across Popperian, Bayesian, error-statistical, Lakatosian, Kuhnian and pragmatist lines, or is the formulation silently choosing one line and calling it the overlap? — Every rule is justified by the sentence that it holds what these lines share, so if no shared core exists, the method's justification turns from inheritance into a choice that has to be defended.
2. Should the method be put to its own test, stating "rules admitted by the method are withdrawn less often than rules admitted without it" with a measure and a threshold recorded beforehand, and what outcome would count against it? — A procedure that demands refutable claims while declaring itself a convention is either exempt by decision or open to its own test, and the answer settles which standing the whole method holds.
3. Where does the method separate the standing of a claim from the decision to act on it under uncertainty, with costs, reversibility and the price of being wrong, and where must expected value, regret analysis or the value of information take over from the acceptance path? — Most of the tasks beyond admitting skill rules are choices that cannot wait for a test, and this boundary decides whether the method extends to them or must hand them to another procedure.
4. If every failed test indicts a bundle — the claim, the prompt, the measure, the harness, the model version, the grader — what does "refuted" pick out, and what must change in the step from "failed repeatably" to "refuted" so that the auxiliaries are named? — In agent evaluation the auxiliaries change more often than the claims, so unless the method names which part of the bundle a failure refutes, its refutations may withdraw the wrong thing.
5. When the author of a hypothesis, the system tested and the judge of the result are all language models, does the procedure change kind from an empirical test to a self-consistency check, and which observation from outside the models would turn it back into a test? — The repository already runs this configuration, so the answer decides whether its records are tests in the sense the method defines or only measure agreement among models.
6. The rules name a comparison, repetitions and a refuting outcome but say nothing about whether the measure measures what the claim is about; if construct validity fails, would any of the seven check questions catch it? — A test can be severe against the wrong quantity, and 2026 work on judge validity shows that a score can track surface features while the record passes every check.
7. Across history, n-of-1 trials, ecology's unreplicable sites and one-off production incidents, is a repeatable, intervening test the right universal requirement, or does the formulation need a second acceptance path for singular claims such as a debugging diagnosis or an appraisal of one study? — Six methods converged on this gap, and the answer decides whether the method covers debugging and appraisal at all or only repeatable claims about rates.
8. Given replication-crisis base rates, undisclosed forking paths, fabricated data and model-written papers in 2026, when does reading a results section reproduce nothing, so that "opened" stands in for "reproduced" under rule 4? — Appraising someone else's research rests entirely on rule 4, and if opening a source carries little evidence, every standing built on an external source has to be weighed again.
9. Does the method say, for each rule, which text it takes the rule from, in which edition and on which page, and where it departs from the source — and checked by opening each passage and quoting it beside the rule, do the cited texts say what the method takes from them? — The method holds others to opening a source before relying on it, and whether it meets that standard for its own pedigree settles the third decision and how far its authority can be trusted.
10. Should a rule periodically re-earn its standing by a fresh test tied to the model version in its bounds, and would such a scheduled expiry change the standings more than any change to the seven rules would? — Model versions are retired within months, so the time-scale of revision may matter more for the truth of the rule list than any refinement of what counts as a test.

## Open Terrain
> Dimensions still underexplored after all agents ran.

- The method's actual record in this repository: questions about how many rules hold which standing, how often refutations occur and which past errors the checks would have caught can only be sharpened once the records are counted, and no agent had that tally to question from.
- Primary passages of the texts the method follows: the questions name Mill's chapter on hypotheses, Duhem, Quine, Lakatos and Mayo's page references, but no passage has been opened and quoted beside a rule, so the sharper questions about where the method departs from each text cannot yet be written.
- Non-Anglophone methodology of science: German Wissenschaftstheorie, Soviet and Russian methodology and French historical epistemology are named as a challenge to the shared-core claim, but no text has been located, and English-language search does not reach their definitions of "law" and "hypothesis".
- How a hypothesis worth testing is formed: the formulation covers acceptance and is nearly silent on discovery, and the literature that rejects the split between the contexts of discovery and justification offers no agreed procedure from which questions could be built.
- Agents as readers and appliers of the method: whether an agent following the skill classifies statements, reads bounds and applies standings as the text intends is asked only in passing, because questioning it well requires watching real sessions rather than reading the formulation.
- Concrete statistical procedures for small-sample agent evaluation: the map asks repeatedly which test decides "nine of ten against four of ten", but it does not set exact tests, Bayesian beta-binomial models, e-values and sequential bounds against each other, since framing that comparison takes knowledge of the answers that question generation does not supply.
- Cross-domain analogs beyond law, medicine, auditing, history and ecology: safety assurance cases, intelligence analysis, forecasting calibration, quantitative finance and metrology each received one fill-in question, and their practices are specialised enough that a deeper set needs someone who works in those fields.
- Developments after November 2026: work on judge construct validity, agentic falsification and benchmark validity moved fast during the months of this scan, so the newest open problems lie beyond what this run could see.
