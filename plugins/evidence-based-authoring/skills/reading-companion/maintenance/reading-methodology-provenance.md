# Provenance of the reading methodology

One line per source reference behind a claim of `references/reading-methodology.md`, keyed by its section (§1 … §13) and the claim, so that the methodology line and its provenance line together carry everything known. Each line holds the citation as it stood in the methodology text, then the claim it backs; the evidence mark (**high**, **moderate**, **low**, **heuristic**) stays on the claim in the methodology. The grouped bibliography the methodology carried closes this file.

## By section

- **§2 Forgetting is interference** · Murre & Dros 2015 — the retention bonus after sleep (~24 h). Wixted 2004, *The psychology and neuroscience of forgetting*, doi:10.1146/annurev.psych.55.090902.141555 (abstract through a summarising fetch of the Europe PMC record) — everyday forgetting is attributed to interference: recently formed memories that have not had a chance to consolidate are open to later mental activity and memory formation, even when that activity is not similar to what was learned; interference from material that shares cues is the form of the list-learning procedures, which the review holds to pertain mainly to the laboratory; sleep improves memory for a recently learned list. Whole books are outside the reviewed procedures, and that similar books read in a row carry both forms is the methodology's inference.
- **§2 The reliable signal of knowledge** · Koriat & Bjork — fluency illusion / foresight bias.
- **§2 A long-term effect without holding the fact** · Sparrow et al. 2011 — transactive / external memory.
- **§3 Most books do not deserve analytical reading** · Adler & Van Doren, *How to Read a Book* — the inspection filter; the four levels and the four analytical questions of the same section name the method.
- **§3 A syntopical set is read for the comparison** · Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn 2013 (the §4.5 line) — comparing cases against studying them one by one. Wiley & Voss 1999, *Constructing arguments from multiple sources: Tasks that promote understanding and not just memory for text*, doi:10.1037/0022-0663.91.2.301 (abstract through a summarising fetch) — two experiments on historical subject matter: students who wrote an argument from several sources, against a narrative, a summary or an explanation, wrote the most integrated essays and did better on inference and analogy tasks. Neither source reads whole books or measures interference. The division of the two rules by what the reader reads for is the session's own reading of them; the name of the mode and its steps follow Adler & Van Doren.
- **§3 Formulate the goal and 3–5 questions** · Pan & Carpenter 2023, *Prequestioning and Pretesting Effects: A Review of Empirical Research, Theoretical Perspectives, and Implications for Educational Practice*, doi:10.1007/s10648-023-09814-5 (abstract through a summarising fetch of the ERIC record EJ1393374) — testing students on information they do not know, with an opportunity to study the correct answers afterwards, can often enhance learning; the benefit may vary with the procedure and with how learning is assessed. The review's questions are posed to the learner; questions a reader writes for a task of their own are outside it, which is why the methodology marks that step an extrapolation.
- **§3 Speed reading is refuted** · Rayner et al. 2016 — the speed–accuracy trade-off; RSVP/Spritz worsen comprehension above ~250 wpm.
- **§4 Only two techniques of ten have high utility** · Dunlosky et al. 2013 (PSPI) — retrieval practice and distributed practice high; highlighting and rereading at the bottom.
- **§4.1 Read by chapters with retrieval** · Roediger & Karpicke 2006 — over 2 days rereading lost ~56% of reproducible material, testing ~13%.
- **§4.2 Feynman check** · Dunlosky et al. 2013 — self-explanation rated moderate.
- **§4.5 Questions that ask to apply** · Agarwal 2019, *Retrieval Practice & Bloom's Taxonomy: Do Students Need Fact Knowledge Before Higher Order Learning?*, doi:10.1037/edu0000282 (abstract) — higher order and mixed quizzes improved higher order test performance, fact quizzes did not; middle school and college students.
- **§4.5 A prediction** · Brod 2021, *Predicting as a learning strategy*, doi:10.3758/s13423-021-01904-1 (full text through a summarising fetch) — a response given with some confidence and followed by immediate feedback; the benefit runs through surprise at an incorrect prediction; the review covers simple declarative material and reports no pooled effect.
- **§4.5 A comparison** · Alfieri, Nokes-Malach & Schunn 2013, *Learning Through Case Comparisons: A Meta-Analytic Review*, doi:10.1080/00461520.2013.775712 (abstract) — d = 0.50, 95 % CI [0.44, 0.56], 57 experiments, 336 tests; larger when learners are asked for similarities, given the principle after the comparison and tested at once. The four forms themselves are the session's wording after one explanation page whose 22 questions were of these kinds; no reader had answered them when the claim was written.
- **§4 Desirable difficulty** · Bjork & Bjork — retrieval, interleaving and generation improve long-term retention at the price of visible slowing.
- **§5.1 Distribute the reviews** · Cepeda et al. 2006 — distributed reviews beat massed ones at equal cost.
- **§5.2 Let at least one sleep pass** · Diekelmann & Born 2010 — consolidation in sleep, resistance to interference.
- **§5.4 Retell it to others** · Nestojko et al. 2014 — the expectation of teaching yields fuller, better-organized recall.
- **§5.4 Retell it to others** · Fiorella & Mayer 2013 — on a delayed test the best are those who actually taught.
- **§6 Three note types** · Ahrens / Luhmann frame — practical, not controlled-verified.
- **§6 The critical invariant** · house design, not a tested protocol: the step at which a note becomes the reader's own (step 5) and the AI's part at each step apply the generation effect of the same section (Slamecka & Graf 1978, in the bibliography below) and the offloading signals of §7 (Kosmyna et al. 2025, preprint; Barcaui 2025). No source tests the pipeline or its step boundaries.
- **§7 Why the line falls exactly here** · MIT EEG (Kosmyna et al. 2025, preprint); Barcaui 2025 (RCT, preliminary) — the cognitive-offloading signals.
- **§8 Implementation intentions** · Gollwitzer & Sheeran 2006 — self-examination by 100% of if-then planners against 53% with intention alone; meta-analysis d ≈ 0.65.
- **§9 The habit period** · Lally et al. 2010 — median ~66 days to automaticity, spread 18–254.
- **§10 The Matthew effect** · Stanovich 1986 — the reinforcing loop of background and reading.
- **§12 Far transfer is contested** · Barnett & Ceci vs Detterman — the century-long dispute.
- **§12 Retrieval practice on complex conceptual material** · van Gog & Sweller vs Karpicke & Aue — the dispute.
- **§13 Constructing and self-correcting** · Chi & Wylie 2014 — ICAP, generating outranks receiving.
- **§13 Constructing and self-correcting** · Bisra et al. 2018 — self-explanation g ≈ 0.55; self-generated prompts beat ready-made explanations.
- **§13.1 Diagnose first** · van de Pol et al. 2010 — the contingent-teaching cycle diagnose → check → intervene → check.
- **§13.3 One graduated, contingent question** · Wood, Bruner & Ross 1976 — the contingent-shift principle.
- **§13.3 One graduated, contingent question** · Chi et al. 2001 — tutors suppressed to prompts still produced learning.
- **§13.3 Default to under-helping** · van de Pol et al. 2015 — frequent help erases the benefit.
- **§13.4 Feedback elaborates** · Van der Kleij, Feskens & Eggen 2015, doi:10.3102/0034654314564881 (abstract) — feedback in computer-based learning environments, 70 effect sizes from 40 studies: knowledge-of-results 0.05, knowledge of the correct response 0.32, elaborated 0.49.
- **§13.5 Never a quiz** · Herbel-Eisenmann & Breyfogle 2005; Mehan 1979 — funneling / the IRE triad, qualitative.
- **§13.5 Never a quiz** · Kluger & DeNisi 1996, doi:10.1037/0033-2909.119.2.254 (abstract, first page of the scanned article) — 607 effect sizes, 23,663 observations: over 1/3 of the feedback interventions decreased performance, and "effectiveness decreases as attention moves up the hierarchy closer to the self and away from the task"; the share belongs to feedback interventions as a whole, the attention to the self is the paper's moderator; the same source backs the feedback-locus mechanism of §13 Honest limits.
- **§13.5 Never a quiz** · Roll et al. 2011 — graded hint ladders get gamed for answers.
- **§13.6a Know when to simply explain** · Sweller & Cooper 1985; Atkinson et al. 2003 — the worked-example effect, d ≈ 0.55.
- **§13.6b Know when to simply explain** · Sinha & Kapur 2021; Schwartz & Bransford 1998 — "a time for telling" / productive failure, observed g ≈ 0.36.
- **§13.6 Unguided struggle** · Kirschner, Sweller & Clark 2006 — unguided struggle without footing overloads working memory.
- **§13.7 Stop re-explaining** · Kalyuga et al. 2003 — expertise reversal.
- **§13 Honest limits** · Bloom — the "2-sigma" tutoring figure; VanLehn 2011 — realistic one-to-one gains d ≈ 0.4–0.8.
