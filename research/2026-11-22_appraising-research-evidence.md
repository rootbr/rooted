# What the rules and techniques of appraising-research rest on — sources re-opened and evidence found to 2026-11-22

The skill appraising-research appraises a finished research report with seven critique methods run as separate agents, an adjudicator, a debate and a synthesis, and its provenance map holds 34 lines, one per source reference behind a design rule or a critique technique. Every source the lines cite was opened again or read through a named substitute. For every line a researcher stated the claim behind the rule, searched for tests of that claim and for documents that prescribe the rule, and closed the line with a record by the scientific method. The records hold 23 decisions of the repository owner and 11 norms, and no line has the standing supported. The claims behind the decisions wait as 19 hypotheses that name 89 tests, and none of those tests ran inside the scope of the claims, a Claude critic appraising a research report. The re-opening found 36 places where the repository's text says other than its source: 9 in the provenance lines, which now state what the sources hold, and 27 in the run set, for which 22 edits wait for the owner. The researchers raised 75 proposals, and a skeptic who re-read each against its source let 24 stand as stated, judged 51 weaker than stated and let none fall. By the skeptic's reading 13 bear against a rule or technique as the skill uses it, 3 bear in a claim's direction, 18 bear on the same question under other conditions, 39 are tested findings no line covers and 2 bear on no line.

- No design rule or critique technique of the skill rests on a test run inside its scope: the 23 decisions are in force by the owner's word, and each of the 19 hypotheses behind them names tests run on other tasks, other models or people.
- The 11 norm lines rest on documents opened again with their issuer and version, and a norm shows that a practice is accepted, not that it makes an appraisal find more of a report's real weaknesses.
- The commonest discrepancy states a source's finding more broadly than the source does: the premortem's figure of about 30% counts reasons rather than correct ones, Lundh et al.'s ratios hold for drug and device studies, and the Constitutional AI paper has no arm without principles.
- The test nearest the skill's conditions, Claude Opus 4.6 reviewing its own artifacts through Claude Code, found a fresh-session review not significantly ahead of one same-session review (F1 28.6 against 27.1, Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Holm-adjusted p = 0.26), which bears against the separate-context rule under other conditions.
- Among the tested findings no line covers, a false prior verdict stated in a tool-using judge's input overturned up to 40% of its correct verdicts, and Claude Code with retrieval available retrieved no cited paper in the analysed astrophysics traces of a peer-review benchmark.
- The run set is unchanged, and the owner's one decision is whether to apply the 22 prepared edits.

## What was investigated and why

The skill's run set is `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/SKILL.md`, the prompts under `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/agents/` and the files under `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/references/`. Seven critique methods run as separate agents: c01 evidence grading, c02 source integrity, c03 assumption excavation, c04 competing hypotheses, c05 premortem and red team, c06 beneficiary and funding, and c07 logic and fallacy. The adjudicator ranks their findings, the debate settles a contested finding the report's conclusion depends on, and the synthesis revises the confidence in the report's main conclusions.

The provenance map, `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/maintenance/appraisal-methodology-provenance.md`, holds one line per source reference behind a claim of the run set: 4 of SKILL.md, 2 of the debate prompt `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/agents/debate/prompt.md` and 28 of `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/references/appraisal-methodology.md`. Twelve lines carry design rules: that a critic needs external grounding against self-correction, sycophancy and self-preference, stated in the methodology and again in SKILL.md; retrieval grounding as design invariant 3; the rubric-driven design; the adversarial debate protocol and the instructions of its judge; and the depth_budget caps on debated findings. Twenty-two lines carry the critique techniques of c01 to c07.

By the method in `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/scientific-method/references/method.md`, a rule of a skill is a prescription with two premises: a goal, which is its owner's decision, and the claim that the technique serves the goal, which its test examines. A citation on a line names a source; it does not say whether the source tests that claim, prescribes the practice, or says what the run set attributes to it.

Two questions were asked of each line. The first: does the cited source say what the line and the run set say it says? The second: which test or document bears on the claim that the rule or technique makes an appraisal find the report's real weaknesses, or avoid raising false ones?

The run does not answer whether any rule or technique makes the skill's own appraisals better, since no test was run with the skill's agents. It does not decide whether the prepared edits enter the run set. It covers the 34 lines the map holds and no statement of the run set that carries no line.

## Method

For every line a researcher opened again the sources the line cites. A book or a paywalled article was read through a library record, a publisher's page or a text that quotes it, and the line says which; 44 sources stayed unopened or were read through another text in this way. The words and figures the lines quote stand in `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/maintenance/citations.json`, 1,676 of them with their sources. Run over the texts fetched on 2026-11-22, `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/scientific-method/maintenance/scripts/check_quotes.py` finds every one in its source.

Researchers took one or two lines each. For every line the researcher stated the claim behind the rule or technique, searched for tests of it and for documents that prescribe it, and wrote the line's text and its record in the format of `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/scientific-method/references/record-format.md`. Where the record is a decision, its claim stands as a hypothesis in `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/maintenance/pending-evidence.md`, with its measure, the work it is compared against, its scope, the outcome that would refute it, the tests found, what each showed and under which conditions.

The method sets the terms of the records. A norm is what an accepted document prescribes, and its record carries the issuer, the version and the locator of the document opened; a norm shows that a practice is accepted, and whether the practice works better is a separate claim. A test supports a claim about a technique's benefit only when it compares the same work with and without the technique, by a measure that follows the property claimed, and could have come out against the claim. A test run on other models, other tasks or people bears on the claim as a transfer and supports nothing inside the claim's scope. A rule needed before its test exists is in force as its owner's decision, and its claim waits as a hypothesis.

Four lines state at a second place of the run set the claim of another line: the three lines of the load-bearing design rule in SKILL.md and the opening paragraph of the debate prompt. Each takes that line's record and hypothesis. The line of the adversarial debate protocol names tests of debate beside the 2009 Tradecraft Primer, which prescribes Team A/Team B; its record is therefore a decision with a hypothesis that names those tests, and the primer stands in its text.

By the method a record with the standing supported goes to an independent reader who answers the check questions of the seven rules. No line took that standing, so no record went to that reader.

A skeptic re-read all 75 proposals against their sources. For each the skeptic recorded what the test compared and found, a verdict (stands as stated, weaker than stated, or fell) and a bearing: against a rule or technique as the skill uses it, in a claim's direction, the same question under other conditions, a tested finding no line covers, or no line. This note gives every proposal in the skeptic's wording and with the skeptic's bearing.

Then 26 lines went back to a writer with the skeptic's reading, and 25 were corrected. Every place where the repository's text says other than its source was recorded with the source's own words: 36 places, 9 in the provenance lines, corrected there, and 27 in the run set, which is unchanged.

The run has five limits. One researcher worked each line, and the search for each line ran within a budget, so a test outside its results is not excluded. Many of the sources are preprints of 2026 not certified by peer review; by the method they count once opened and the test is found in them, and a few were read by the abstract alone. The researchers, the writers and the skeptic are models of one model family, so an error that family shares is not caught by the skeptic. No test ran inside the scope of the claims: the skill's critics, on a 2026 Claude model, appraising a research report.

## What the re-opened sources say

Of the 36 places where the repository's text says other than its source, 9 stand in the provenance lines and 27 in the run set. They fall into four kinds. In the first, a title or a bibliographic detail differs from what the source prints: the title of Zheng et al. carries "and Chatbot Arena", Wineburg and McGrew's working paper is No 2017.A1 of September 2017, and Heuer and Pherson's book is Structured Analytic Techniques for Intelligence Analysis (CQ Press, 2010). In the second, a figure stands beside a measure or a source other than the one that reports it. In the third, a source's finding is stated more broadly than the source states it. In the fourth, a quotation, a list or a term is given in other words than the source's: the CRAAP handout names five purposes where the run set names three, SIFT's fourth move names no statistics, and the 2008 GRADE article speaks of the quality of evidence where the run set speaks of certainty.

For the run set 22 exact edits in 3 files are prepared: 6 change a figure, a term or a quotation, and 16 change the statement of what a source shows. Twenty stand in references/appraisal-methodology.md, one in `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/agents/method-c01-evidence-grading/prompt.md` and one in `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/appraising-research/agents/method-c06-beneficiary-funding/prompt.md`. Six examples show the kinds.

- The premortem. The run set's § c05 Premortem says that prospective hindsight raises the ability to correctly identify reasons for a future outcome by about 30%. Veinott, Klein and Wiggins (2010) give the 1989 effect as having "increased the number of reasons for the event's occurrence by approximately 30%", and the abstract of Mitchell, Russo and Pennington (1989) reports that temporal perspective showed little influence while outcome uncertainty strongly affected the explanations. The provenance line states the figure as a count of reasons drawn by an event stated as certain; the edit of the run set waits.
- The sponsorship ratios. The run set's § c06 Sponsorship-bias check and the c06 prompt give RR 1.27 and 1.34 for industry-sponsored or industry-funded work in general. Lundh et al. (2017) study industry-sponsored drug and device studies, give 1.27 for favourable efficacy results and 1.34 for favourable conclusions, and find harms results similar (RR 1.37, 95% CI 0.64 to 2.93). The restriction to low risk of bias concerns 14 included meta-research papers, and its subgroup difference is P = 0.03 for efficacy results and P = 0.60 for conclusions, with the association for harms reversed (RR 0.82). The provenance line names efficacy results; one edit in each file waits.
- The key assumptions. The run set's § c03 Key Assumptions Check says that roughly one in four assumptions collapses under written scrutiny. Pherson's 2005 paper names no rate, and his 2009 paper gives the figure as "the author's experience in conducting Key Assumptions Check exercises over the past decade", for key assumptions initially presumed true, with no count. The provenance line cites the 2009 paper for the figure; the edit waits.
- Self-preference. The run set's § Why a critic needs external grounding says that a model rates text in its own style, or that it produced, more favourably. Zheng et al. measure position and verbosity bias and write that "our study cannot determine whether the models exhibit a self-enhancement bias". The provenance line says so; the edit waits.
- Constitutional AI. The run set's § rubric-driven design says that an explicit written set of principles is what makes model self-critique steerable and reliable. Bai et al. have no arm without principles, report that "the number of constitutions does not appear to have a significant effect on harmlessness score", and found that the critiques "often made inaccurate or overstated criticisms". The edit waits.
- The ladder of designs. The run set's § c01 Hierarchy of evidence and the c01 prompt place case-control studies above case series and end the ladder with mechanistic reasoning or expert opinion. The Oxford 2011 table puts case-series, case-control and historically controlled studies on Step 4 and mechanism-based reasoning alone on Step 5, with no step for expert opinion. One edit in each file waits.

The 22 edits carry the 27 places in the run set: two places share one sentence of § c06 Sponsorship-bias check, and five share one sentence of § c07 Paul-Elder intellectual standards. One of the edits concerns the ranking of causal viewpoints. The run set's § c03 Correlation vs. causation calls specificity and analogy the weakest of Hill's viewpoints, while Hill ranks none of his nine viewpoints and the ranking of specificity as weak is the EPA's (2005, § 2.2.1.7), which, like Hill, calls analogy none of the weakest.

The map closes with six verification flags, cautions on citations not confirmed first-hand. Three are settled by the re-opening and state what the sources hold: the Miniature Guide gives nine standards on p. 12 and ten on p. 21 and the Foundation's web page eight; the run set uses the seven-domain ROBINS-I list of 2016, and a draft V2 posted on 2025-11-20 restructures the domains; Mitchell, Russo and Pennington were read by their abstract, and the page range stands as Klein's attribution gives it. Three stand as cautions: the wording of Walton's critical questions for argument from popular opinion and from cause to effect, the Herzog and Hertwig figure, and the figure that superforecasters did about 30% better than intelligence officers.

## The norms

Eleven lines record a norm. Each document below prescribes the practice the technique applies; a norm shows that the practice is accepted, and the tests found beside it bear on whether it works.

- GRADE certainty rating. The GRADE Working Group's 2008 article prescribes four levels of quality of evidence, high, moderate, low and very low, with randomised trials beginning as high quality evidence and observational studies as low, five reasons to rate down and three to rate up (§ How does the GRADE system classify quality of evidence? and Box 2).
- Hierarchy of evidence. The OCEBM 2011 table, in its row "Does this intervention help? (Treatment Benefits)", ranks a systematic review of randomized trials or n-of-1 trials first and mechanism-based reasoning last, with case-series, case-control and historically controlled studies on one step and no step for expert opinion. Its Introductory Document states that the levels are a hierarchy of the likely best evidence and not a definitive judgment of its quality, and that lower-level evidence can be the stronger.
- Risk-of-bias signal. The RoB 2 guidance of 22 August 2019 prescribes five domains for a result of a randomized trial: the randomization process, deviations from intended interventions, missing outcome data, measurement of the outcome and selection of the reported result. ROBINS-I (BMJ 2016) prescribes seven domains for studies that did not use randomisation: confounding and selection of participants before the intervention, classification of interventions at it, and four domains after it. The draft ROBINS-I V2 posted on 20 November 2025 drops the domain on deviations from intended interventions and renumbers the others.
- Quality-of-Information Check. The 2009 Tradecraft Primer prescribes a check that "Evaluates completeness and soundness of available information sources", in steps from reviewing all sources for accuracy and checking the corroboration of critical reporting to indicating a level of confidence in the sources (pp. 10–11), and reports no test.
- Key Assumptions Check. The primer prescribes articulating every stated and unstated premise of the analytic line, challenging each, and considering under what conditions those that must be true might not hold (The Method, steps 1–4, pp. 7–9).
- Correlation vs. causation. The EPA's Guidelines for Carcinogen Risk Assessment prescribe nine aspects adapted from Hill "as an aid in judging causality", call specificity "one of the weaker guidelines for causality" and temporality "among the strongest criteria for an inference of causality", and state that the aspects "cannot be used as a strictly quantitative checklist" (§ 2.2.1.7).
- Devil's advocacy, red team and what-if. The primer prescribes Devil's Advocacy, which builds the "best possible case for an alternative" explanation, "What If?" Analysis, which "Assumes that an event has occurred" and has the analyst "Think backwards" from it, and Red Team Analysis against the "mirror-image" problem, and it allows that an "individual analyst can often assume the" role of the devil's advocate (pp. 17, 24 and 31).
- Sponsorship-bias check. The Cochrane Handbook, version 6.5, chapter 7, prescribes that review authors consider the source of funding and the conflicts of interest of the authors of each included study, and that they not judge a risk-of-bias domain higher only because its investigators have conflicts of interest (§ 7.8 and MECIR Box 7.8.a C59).
- Conflict of interest and purpose. The Meriam Library's CRAAP handout dated 8/24/04 asks under Purpose whether the information is there to inform, teach, sell, entertain or persuade, and whether its authors or sponsors make their intentions clear.
- Paul-Elder intellectual standards. The Miniature Guide to Critical Thinking, fourth edition, gives on p. 12 a table of nine universal intellectual standards with three questions each, Significance among them, and on p. 21 lists ten, adding Completeness; the Foundation's web page of October 2010 gives eight as "some of the most essential".
- Internal consistency. ICD 203 prescribes that analytic products "should be internally consistent" (§ D.6.e(6)) and makes its standards "the foundational assessment criteria" for the review of analytic products (§ D.2). Krathwohl (2002, Table 3) lists Checking and Critiquing as cognitive processes under 5.0 Evaluate, "Making judgments based on criteria and standards".

## Tests against a rule or technique

Thirteen proposals bear against a rule or technique as the skill uses it, every one under other conditions than the claim's. The five with the strongest test come first: each names its test, rests on a sample of some size, or runs nearest the skill's conditions.

The separate-context rule, on the line appraisal-methodology § Why a critic needs external grounding — self-preference and its second place in SKILL.md. Song (arXiv:2603.12123v2, Tables 2–3 and 6) had Claude Opus 4.6 generate 30 artifacts, 10 Python modules, 10 technical tutorials and 10 presentation scripts with 5 planted errors each, and review them through the Claude Code Agent tool in four conditions, three runs each: in the generating session with its full history, in a second pass of that session, as a fresh sub-agent given the request, and as a fresh session given the artifact alone. By a three-run artifact-level Wilcoxon signed-rank test, Holm-adjusted over three comparisons, the fresh session's F1 of 28.6 stood against 27.1 for the same-session review (p = 0.26), 23.8 for the sub-agent (p = 0.057) and 21.7 for the second same-session pass (p = 0.004). On recall, the line's own measure, the same-session review stood at 28.3 against 27.1, and the source names no test for that pair. The skeptic let the proposal stand, against the rule under other conditions: code, tutorials and scripts reviewed with a generic prompt rather than the skill's critique methods on a research report, with the model and the harness matching. The skeptic found missing that the primary analysis excludes the third same-session run, with which the fresh session beats it at p = 0.043, that run 1 alone favoured the fresh session (paired t, p = 0.008), that the author calls the remaining same-model records unverified rather than known to be clean, and that the companion preprint (arXiv:2610.01471v2, Table 2) labels the same p = 0.26 unadjusted.

The rubric-driven design, on the line appraisal-methodology § rubric-driven design. Asgari et al. (arXiv:2610.24971, §4.1–4.2) had Claude Haiku, Sonnet and Opus 5.5 grade outputs pass or fail: 72 security-reconnaissance reports, 24 correct and 48 flawed, and the human-labelled SummEdits (146) and RAGTruth (160) sets, under a free-form verdict, a generic 1–5 scale, hand-written checks or generated checks. Hand-written checks passed 95.8–100% of correct reports across judges and generated checks 0–4.2%, while a free-form verdict passed every correct report under every judge and Sonnet's free-form verdict passed 100% of the reports with a missed finding; the source names no test of these differences. On RAGTruth a free-form verdict passed 80.6% of faithful responses and caught 88.1% of hallucinated ones, against 41.2% and 95.0% with ten generic checks, which failed 29 and 34 faithful responses that the free-form verdict passed and passed none that it failed (exact McNemar test, p < 10⁻⁸ in each of two runs). ## Open questions

- No hypothesis lacks a test found: each of the 19 names at least one. None names a test inside its scope, so every one waits for a comparison of the skill's own critic with and without its step.
- The 11 norm lines register no hypothesis. Whether GRADE, the OCEBM ladder, the RoB 2 and ROBINS-I domains, the primer's three checks, the EPA's aspects, the Cochrane funding check, CRAAP-Purpose, the Paul-Elder standards and the consistency standard of ICD 203 make the skill's critics find more real weaknesses is a claim no record states, and the tests found beside these lines ran on people, other models or other tasks.
- 44 sources stayed unopened or were read through another text. Among them are Heuer and Pherson's Structured Analytic Techniques, read through its library record and through notes that quote its §9.5; Schwenk's meta-analysis of devil's advocacy, a lead not opened; Lehner et al. on confirmation bias, read as Dhami et al. report it; Gallop et al. on the premortem, read through course notes; Caramancion on the CRAAP Test, read as Ratcliffe reports it; Klein's article, read through course notes; the body of Walton's Fundamentals of Critical Argumentation, of which only the front matter was read; and the abstracts alone of Mitchell, Russo and Pennington, of Nemeth, Brown and Rogers, of Buffel du Vaure et al., of Faltinsen et al., of Mustafa et al., of Toews et al. and of Nyrhi et al.
- Three verification flags of the map stand as cautions: the exact wording of Walton's critical questions for argument from popular opinion and from cause to effect, the dialectical-bootstrapping figure of Herzog and Hertwig (2009), carried as a task figure (error 130.8 to 123.2) rather than a general effect size, and the figure that superforecasters did about 30% better than intelligence officers, which rests on journalistic and Good Judgment Project reporting rather than one controlled effect size.
- The owner's decision on the 22 prepared edits is open.

The tests that would settle the hypotheses have to be run with the skill's own agents. Each needs research reports with planted weaknesses of the kind the step targets, clean copies on which false findings are counted, a blind reader who judges findings false or unfounded, the skill's critics on 2026 Claude models in Claude Code, and the threshold and the runs committed before the result. The comparisons the hypotheses name are these:

- critics in fresh sub-agents against the same methods run in the session that wrote the report;
- the same critics with web search and fetch removed, judging from the report's text and the model's own knowledge, for self-correction, retrieval grounding and claim-source match;
- an assessing framing with the conclusion the author hopes for stated, against the skill's framing;
- a one-sentence critique instruction against the written set of questions;
- every finding that meets the debate selection rule debated with no length target, against the caps of 3, 6 and 10;
- a single critic's ruling on a contested finding against the debate, and the judge prompt without its instructions on evidence, tone, order and spot-check, with the two cases reversed, lengthened or rewritten in a confident tone;
- each technique's critic with its step removed: lateral reading, the SIFT moves, the six critical questions, warrant reconstruction, the ACH procedure, the failure frame, the search for suppressed evidence and base rates, the WYSIATI step, the cui bono step and the fallacy sweep.
