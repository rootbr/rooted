# Indentation, line breaks and spacing show statements and operands grouped as the language parses them

## Thesis
Layout shows the grouping the parser applies. Every statement that indentation, or its place on the header's line, shows inside a control statement's body is inside that body as parsed: a body meant to hold two or more statements is explicitly delimited, and a statement that follows an undelimited single-statement body, or an empty one, starts its own line at the header's level. The one statement an undelimited body holds either shares the header's line or is indented under the header. Each `else` is aligned with the `if` it binds to. A block or an `if` that follows the closing brace of an `if` statement on the same line is joined to it by `else` or starts its own line, and an `else` that follows a closing brace shares its line with the block or the `if` it introduces unless the closing brace, the `else` and the opening brace each stand on a line of their own. A unary operator after an assignment's `=` is not written against the `=` while the `=` is spaced from its left side (`x = -y`, not `x =- y`, which looks like `x -= y`), and a unary operator after a binary operator stays against its own operand instead of sitting against the binary operator while spaced from its operand (`a && !b`, not `a &&! b`, which looks like one operator). Where spacing differs within one expression, the operands written closer together are the ones the language's precedence binds first. The rule holds whether or not the parsed behaviour is the intended one.

## Rationale
Conventional indentation gives strong guidance to a program's structure, and where the parse differs that guidance misleads. It is easy to add a statement under an undelimited single-statement body and forget the delimiters: indented like the body, the new statement reads as part of it, yet it runs unconditionally, or, under a loop, once after it on the last element, a logic error the weakness catalogue lists as incorrect block delimitation. In a nested `if` without braces around its bodies, where the parser ignores indentation, an `else` aligned with the outer `if` binds to the inner one, and where the inner `if` is an authentication check that pairing lets unauthorized users obtain administrator privileges; indentation that disguises what a program does can lead to a security breach. A line at the header's level with neither braces nor indentation leaves unclear which statement is conditional and confuses maintainers, more so once a line in it is commented out. Indentation that mixes tabs and spaces can look right in one editor and misleading in another configured differently. A block or an `if` right after the closing brace of an `if` statement looks like a branch whose `else` is missing, and a line break after `else` can hide that the next block is its branch; both are likely remnants of a refactoring and confuse even when the code is correct. Spacing can turn one operator into the look of another: `=-`, `=!` and `=*` are either typos of `-=`, `!=` and `*=` or confusing, and `&&!` looks like a different operator. Spacing also reads as grouping: a style convention suggests whitespace around the lowest-priority operators of a mixed expression, so that tight spacing marks the operands bound first, and one language documents that `x<<8 + y<<16` means what its spacing implies there, unlike in the other languages. Layout that agrees with the parse removes the misleading guidance: what the reader sees grouped is what the parser groups.

## Example
```python
bad:  total =- step
      found = index ==- 1
      mask = high<<8 + low
good: total -= step
      found = index == -1
      mask = (high << 8) + low
```

## Limits
Where the parser takes the grouping of statements from indentation and rejects tab-and-space mixing that makes the meaning depend on the width of a tab, indentation cannot disagree with the parse, and only the operator and spacing checks apply. An undelimited single-statement body on the header's line or indented under it, with the next statement starting its own line at the header's level, shows its parse and is outside the rule; whether every body takes braces is a brace convention, not a disagreement between layout and parse. A brace style that puts the closing brace, the `else` and the opening brace of an `else` block each on a line of its own shows the pairing and is outside the rule. Whether an expression varies its spacing by precedence is a matter of judgment: uniform spacing around every operator of an expression implies no grouping and is outside the rule, and spacing that matches the precedence of the file's language is truthful even where another language would group the same text differently. An `=` that binds a keyword argument or a parameter default is not an assignment, and a style convention writes it without spaces for a keyword argument and for the default of an unannotated parameter. Indentation width, line length, blank lines and brace placement beyond the pairing of `else` are outside the rule. A tolerance stated in `project_context` that exempts generated files rejects the finding for those files.

## Validator
Grep the added lines for a control header whose line ends without an opening brace or carries its body after the condition (`if (...)`, `for (...)`, `while (...)`, a bare `else`), a closing brace followed on its line by `{` or `if`, an `=` with a space before it and a unary operator against it, a binary operator followed directly by a unary operator that is spaced from its operand, and an expression whose operators are spaced unevenly. For a control header, read the hunk's following lines: the parsed body of an undelimited header is its one next statement, or an empty statement when the header ends in a terminator. Check that every line indented deeper than the header, up to the next line at the header's level, belongs to that body; that a body on a line of its own is indented under the header; that no statement follows the body on the header's line; and that each `else` is aligned with the `if` it binds to, the lexically nearest preceding `if` the syntax allows. Where the indentation mixes tabs and spaces, check it at more than one tab width. For a closing brace followed by `{` or `if`, check whether the brace ends an `if` statement; for an `else` that follows a closing brace, ends its line and introduces a block or an `if`, check whether the closing brace, the `else` and the opening brace each stand on a line of their own. For an operator hit, skip a keyword argument and a parameter default. For uneven spacing, group the expression by the precedence of the file's language and compare that grouping with the one the spacing implies. Validator question: **Does an added line's indentation, line placement or operator spacing show a statement inside or outside a control body, an `else` paired with an `if`, a compound operator, or a grouping of operands that differs from what the file's language parses?** Yes → flag.

## Finding output
When the validator answers yes, the finder emits one finding (`rule_id: CODE-50`, severity major, `file`, `symbol`, `code` = the control header with the misleading lines, or the misleading expression, quoted verbatim from the diff, `fix` = the same code with layout and parse in agreement — the body delimited or the stray line moved to the header's level, the `else` added, realigned or joined to its block, the operators spaced as they parse, the compound operator the spacing suggests, or the operands parenthesized — in the file's language, `rationale` = naming what the layout shows and what the language parses instead).
