Dear Dana,

The first release of the arrival feed for your stop displays is due on 30 November 2026. Three points still need a decision from your side. Each one is described below, with what changes depending on your answer. If you reply with the letter of the option you choose, that is enough.

We need your answers by [date] to keep the 30 November release.

1. A second network connection on your side

Right now the feed reaches your displays over one network connection. If that connection drops, it takes up to about 2.5 seconds to reconnect, and during that time the displays get no new predictions. While the connection is down we store the predictions on our side (up to 40 seconds' worth) and send them as soon as it is back. Your current contract does not include a second connection.

- A. Keep one connection plus our backup storage. Nothing is needed from your team. During a drop, displays can lag by a few seconds and then catch up.
- B. Add a second connection on your side. One of your network engineers would need to spend about a week setting it up, and this goes beyond the current contract. A drop on one connection would not interrupt the displays.

Which do you prefer: A or B?

---

2. A prediction that is far off from where the bus actually is

Sometimes a predicted arrival time can differ from the bus's actual position, as recorded by your vehicle tracker, by more than 1 minute. We need to know what passengers should see when that happens.

- A. Leave it out. The display shows no time for that bus until a reliable prediction comes in. Passengers may see a gap, but never a time that is badly wrong.
- B. Show it with a mark (for example "approx."). Passengers still see a time, but they are told it may be inaccurate.
- C. Stop the feed for that stop until predictions are reliable again. Nothing wrong is shown, but the whole stop's display goes without times for a while.

Which should passengers see: A, B or C?

---

3. How a "missed prediction" is counted for the service-level agreement

The service-level agreement limits how many predictions may be missed. There are two ways to count a missed one, and the choice changes the figures in the quality reports we send you and the point at which an alert is raised.

- A. By message: a prediction counts as missed when one of the numbered messages in the sequence never arrives. This is exact, but a few seconds without any messages counts only as the messages that were actually lost.
- B. By time: a prediction counts as missed when a display gets no fresh prediction within a set time. This is closer to what a passenger sees, but it depends on the time limit we agree on.

How should missed predictions be counted: A (by message) or B (by time)?
