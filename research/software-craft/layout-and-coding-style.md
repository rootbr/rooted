# Research note — Layout and coding style

Topic `layout-and-coding-style` of group A. Code inside a function, domain `code`, id prefix `CODE`. Formulation locators: CC31 (Code Complete 2nd ed. ch. 31 'Layout and Style'); SEG8 (Software Engineering at Google ch. 8 'Style Guides and Rules'); PoP1 (The Practice of Programming ch. 1 'Style'); the 97 Things essays 'Code Layout Matters' (thing 13) and 'Automate Your Coding Standard' (thing 4).

Code Complete ch. 31, SEG ch. 8, The Practice of Programming ch. 1 and the 97 Things essays 13 and 4 agree on four positions. Layout shows the structure the code really has and never misleads. Consistency matters more than any particular choice (SEG8: indentation width and line limits have 'no clear, measurable, technical benefit'). Blank lines separate logical paragraphs, sparingly. A language's standard formatter settles the mechanical choices. The industry disagrees on the concrete parameters, so those go to the language corpora: the line limit (79, 80, 88, 99, 100, 120, or none in Go), tabs or spaces and the indent width, braces on single-statement bodies in Java and TypeScript (always, or allowed on the header's line), the operator's side at a wrap (Go must break after it; Java, Rust and PEP 8 before; ESLint after), spacing that shows precedence (gofmt and PEP 8 yes, the Rust style guide no), column alignment (gofmt aligns, PEP 8 forbids it by hand), and whether hand-finished layout may refine formatter output (Freeman and Checkstyle against Effective Go). The cards keep the cross-language core and carry each dispute's separating condition: whether a limit is set, whether a body starts on its own line, the number of clauses, who maintains an alignment, whether the guide requires a point, and whether the formatter documents a skip directive. The evidence is strongest where layout misrepresents the parse. CWE-483, CERT EXP52-J, Sonar S2681 and S3973, and clippy's suspicious-formatting lints all cover it, and Gopstein et al. (FSE 2017, 73 participants) measured more mistakes on code with omitted braces. A local run shows that precedence tables diverge (1 << 2 + 3 is 7 in Go and 32 in Java, TypeScript, Python and Rust). Elsewhere the cards rest on official style documentation and tool rules, because research on spacing is thin and contradictory. Miara et al. (1983) found 2 to 4 spaces best, and Bauer et al. (2019) found no effect of indentation level. The Oliveira et al. review finds most spacing comparisons non-significant, which it attributes to low statistical power. Buse and Weimer's blank-line association is, in the authors' words, descriptive. So no card claims a measured comprehension benefit for an indent width, a blank-line rule or operator spacing, and the only human-subject line-length result is an opinion survey favouring 80 characters (dos Santos and Gerosa 2018, p < 0.001).

## Formulation layer

The canon agrees on four things. Layout must show the code's logical structure and never mislead (CC31's Fundamental Theorem, PoP 'Indent to show structure', the Rust principle 'avoiding misleading formatting'). Consistency matters more than any particular choice: SEG8 says indentation width and line limits have 'no clear, measurable, technical benefit for one form over another'. Blank lines should separate logical paragraphs, sparingly. A language's standard formatter should settle the mechanical choices (Effective Go, Google Go guide, Rust style guide, SEG8 formatters and gofmt case study, 97 Things 4 and 14). The concrete parameters are disputed or settled per language, so they belong to the language corpora rather than to cross-language cards. These are the line limit (79/80/99/100/none/~120), whether to break before or after a binary operator (PEP 8 and Google Java before, CC31 and Google Python's own example after), braces on single-statement bodies (Google Java/TS always, PoP omits them except to settle a dangling else), and alignment (CC31, Clean Code, Google Java and PEP 8 discourage hand alignment, but gofmt aligns columns). Freeman (97 Things 13) dissents from formatter-only layout and asks for hand-finished line breaks and grouping. SEG8 partly concedes this ('formatting a matrix'), while Effective Go says 'don't work around it'. The APoSD-vs-Clean-Code discussion touches layout only on whether ordering methods can make up for entangled methods, where Martin and Ousterhout disagree, and on Martin's 'fit on the same screen' remark. Neither book is openable here. CC31 is reached through third-party notes and a copy of its Layout checklist (relayed), PoP ch. 1 through an unofficial GitHub transcription (relayed, not to be cited as a URL), and Clean Code ch. 5 through third-party notes. CC31's research citations (Miara 1983, Gorla 1990) and Martin's 120-column limit are unfetched memory. WebSearch was out of budget, so no paper abstract was relayed for the indentation studies.

## Reception layer

Reception agrees broadly that layout should be consistent and that a tool, not a person, should enforce it. ESLint deprecated its formatting rules in v8.53 (the rules stay available until v11), typescript-eslint and Ruff send formatting to a dedicated formatter, and gofmt, rustfmt, Black, Prettier, dart_style and Biome all limit their options on purpose to end style debates. The dissent is narrower: Steve Freeman (97 Things #13) wants a formatter for the basics plus hand-finishing for intent, Checkstyle's Indentation page notes that pretty printers 'can't anticipate how format should be done', SEG ch. 8 says humans format a matrix better than a general formatter, and Prettier, Black and Griesemer's gofmt keep the author's blank lines and line breaks. Several thresholds and choices are contested by name. Line length has defaults or recommendations of 79 (PEP 8), 80 (ESLint, Checkstyle, Prettier, Google Python, dart, Linux preferred), 88 (Black, Ruff), 99 (Uber, PEP 8 by team agreement), 100 (Pylint, Google Java, Rust, checkpatch), 120 (golangci-lint lll) and none at all for Go (Effective Go, Google Go guide). Other contested choices: tabs or spaces and indent width (2, 4 or 8 spaces); braces around a single statement (Google Java, Sonar S121, PMD, Checkstyle and CERT EXP52-J require them, while the Linux kernel, Airbnb, Standard and the Google TS and Python guides allow omission or single-line forms); whether a line breaks before or after a binary operator (PEP 8 prefers before, pycodestyle disables both W503 and W504 by default, and Google Python's example breaks after); horizontal alignment (gofmt aligns, PEP 8 forbids extra spaces for it, Google Java permits it and never requires it); and optional grouping parentheses (PMD UselessParentheses against Google Java §4.7). Sources converge without dispute on these points: one statement per line (with single-line-if exceptions); consistency within the file or project first; indentation that must not misrepresent block structure (Sonar S2681 and S3973, clippy suspicious_else_formatting, the dart_style FAQ on goto fail); continuation lines set apart from block bodies (Google Go, PEP 8); and refactoring, such as extracting a variable, before wrapping an over-long line (Google Java, Google Go, rustfmt, dart_style). Go and Rust syntax requires the braces, so the brace rule applies only to Java and TypeScript. WebSearch was exhausted, so HN and Lobsters threads, Hettinger's 'Beyond PEP 8' talk, the Linux commit bdc48fa11e46 message, and the papers (Miara 1983, Buse & Weimer, dos Santos & Gerosa, Allamanis) could not be opened. The one exception is Bauer et al. 2019, whose abstract comes from the authors' GitHub data repository and reports a failure to replicate Miara's indentation effect.

## Evidence layer

At the research level the evidence on layout is thin and partly contradictory. Miara et al. 1983 found that indentation level affects comprehension, with 2 to 4 spaces best. Bauer et al. 2019 (n=22) did not replicate this. Hanenberg et al. 2024 (relayed) again report a large effect of indented versus non-indented code. The Oliveira et al. systematic review (arXiv 2208.12141, full text fetched) finds most spacing comparisons non-significant, which it attributes to low statistical power. It does support 80-character lines and present, own-line block delimiters. The firmest non-research anchors are the brace and misleading-indentation cluster (CERT EXP52-J, CWE-483 via MITRE D3FEND, Sonar S2681, Gopstein et al. FSE 2017, a hands-on Java run) and the official style and formatter documentation (PEP 8, Google Java Style §4, the Rust Style Guide, Effective Go and gofmt). That documentation agrees on several points: formatting by machine, following the style guide and then local consistency, one statement per line, sparing single blank lines, and a column limit of 79 to 100 (except Go, which has none). The canon disagrees on four points: where a wrapped line breaks around an operator (Go must break after the operator, while Java, Python and Rust break before it and ESLint defaults to after), whether spacing should show precedence (gofmt and PEP 8 say yes, the Rust style guide says no), alignment (gofmt aligns columns, while Google Java and PEP 8 discourage hand alignment), and brace placement (dos Santos and Gerosa's subjects preferred own-line braces, against K&R, which Go's grammar enforces). Papers were reachable only through GitHub-hosted abstracts (IEEE metadata mirrors, the ACM SIGSOFT OpenTOC, bibliography files, an arXiv text mirror, an arXiv RSS mirror) because WebSearch was exhausted. The per-practice p-values of dos Santos and Gerosa come from the review text. The quotes from Lee 2015, Hanenberg 2024, Oliveira 2024's conclusion, review v3 and Buse and Weimer §6 come through third-party relays, so none of them may be a card's only anchor. ESLint has deprecated all of its core layout rules since v8.53.0 but kept curly, which itself separates formatting (left to formatters) from brace omission (a bug-prevention check).

## Spine — candidate rules in source order

1. **Indentation, line breaks and spacing never suggest a grouping of statements or operands that the language does not parse** — Layout shows the structure the language actually parses. A line indented as part of a control body is inside that body; an else, or an if that follows a closing brace, visibly continues or visibly ends the preceding if instead of sitting where an else seems to be missing; and spacing never makes one operator look like another (=- for -=, &&! for && !) or implies a precedence the language does not apply. A block meant to hold two or more statements but left undelimited is the weakness CWE-483 (incorrect block delimitation); the CERT Java recommendation EXP52-J shows an authentication branch whose indentation 'disguises the functionality of the program, potentially leading to a security breach', and static analysers flag the pattern in Java (Sonar S2681, S3973) and Rust (clippy's suspicious-formatting lints, which fire even when the code is correct because it 'might look confusing'). Spacing by precedence is truthful only where the language's precedence matches it: Effective Go notes that x<<8 + y<<16 means what the spacing implies in Go, 'unlike in the other languages'.

2. **Blank lines inside a routine are single and sparing, each separating one group of related statements from the next** — Inside a routine body, a single blank line marks the boundary between groups of statements that each carry out one step, and the statements of one group stay together without blank lines between them. Formatters keep the author's single blank lines inside functions, so the grouping is the writer's decision. The measured effect is weak.
