# Cluster 1 — Philosophical lineage and fidelity to the cited texts

All 51 quotations were found by the script (49 `found`, 2 `near` by punctuation); the findings judge what each passage carries in context. Line M-A changed during this run; F1.9 judges the newer text.

## Findings

### F1.1 The passages cited for the method's opening deny a shared core, so method.md's sentence "They hold what the main lines of the philosophy of science share" is contradicted by its own sources

- Answers: 1, 2, 18; frontier 1, frontier 9
- Evidence:
  - Hoyningen-Huene in SEP "Scientific Method" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-method/ — full text — §7, the paragraph cited at M-0/2 — "there is no longer any consensus among philosophers and historians of science about the nature of science"; "what has been left to characterize science is only its fallibility"
  - same entry, §1 — "The lack of a singular method across all disciplines of science is a reason for its reliability as a practice, not a mark against it."
  - SEP "Science and Pseudo-Science" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/pseudo-science/ — full text — §8, cited at M-0/3–4 — "Philosophers and other theoreticians of science differ widely in their views on what science is."; "almost complete disagreement on the general criteria that these judgments should presumably be based upon"
- Standing: two or more independent sources
- For the method (my inference): wrong — these passages carry the claim that the rules are a convention. They contradict the claim that the lines share them: the agreement they report is on particular verdicts, and the seven rules are general criteria. It should present the rules as a reasoned choice among the lines.

### F1.2 Named lines reject named rules: Kuhn and Lakatos reject refutation as what withdraws a theory (rule 7); Bayesians set acceptance aside (the standings) and hold the stopping plan irrelevant (rule 3)

- Answers: 2, 19; frontier 1
- Evidence:
  - SEP "Thomas Kuhn" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/thomas-kuhn/ — full text — on normal science — "Nor do they regard anomalous results as falsifying those theories."; "Kuhn’s account argues that resisting falsification is precisely what every disciplinary matrix in science does."
  - SEP "Imre Lakatos" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/lakatos/ — full text — §2.2, cited at M-7/5 — "All theories, in this sense, are born refuted and die refuted.", then "the unit of scientific evaluation is no longer the individual theory (as with Popper), but the sequence of theories, the research programme"
  - SEP "Bayesian Epistemology" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/epistemology-bayesian/ — full text — on inductive inference, on whether a credence suffices for "believing or accepting" — "But to many Bayesians, that issue seems only secondary, or better forgone as argued by Jeffrey (1970)."
  - SEP "Philosophy of Statistics" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/statistics/ — full text — §3.2.2 — "Some will find this dependence unacceptable: the intentions and plans of the researcher are irrelevant to the evidential value of the data."
- Standing: two or more independent sources
- For the method (my inference): the text says otherwise — M-7 cites Lakatos for revision, but the passage denies that refutation settles a theory's fate. Rule 3's "decided before the test" takes the error-statistical side of the stopping-rule dispute. Both should be recorded as choices.

### F1.3 Rule 6's ground, that a later observation tests the hypothesis and the observation it grew from does not, is predictivism, which is disputed; Mill, whom the map cites for "hypothesis", rejects it

- Answers: 2, 10, 18, 23; frontier 1, frontier 9
- Evidence:
  - SEP "Prediction versus Accommodation" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/prediction-accommodation/ — full text — §1 and §7 — Mill "categorically denied this claim"; Keynes: "The peculiar virtue of prediction or predesignation is altogether imaginary"
  - Mill, *A System of Logic*, 7th ed. (1868), III.xiv §6 — http://davidmhart.com/liberty/EnglishClassicalLiberals/MilJS/1868-SystemLogic/Mill_SystemLogic2.html — full text — "Such predictions and their fulfilment are, indeed, well calculated to impress the uninformed"; "Though twenty such coincidences should occur, they would not prove the reality of the undulatory ether"
  - Devezer et al. 2021, RSOS 8:200805 — https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8101540/ — full text — section on preregistration — "Statistical rigor can neither be ensured by preregistration nor would be compromised by not preregistering a plan."
  - Wagenmakers 2021 (author's blog, revised 2021-03-25) — https://www.bayesianspectacles.org/the-torture-of-straw-men-a-critical-impression-of-devezer-et-al-the-case-for-formal-methodology-in-scientific-reform/ — full text — Tldr — "it is possible to provide a statistical underpinning for the idea that data-dredging differs from foresight, both in the frequentist and in the Bayesian paradigm"
- Standing: disputed: predictivists (Popper, Lakatos, use-novelty) hold that a prediction outweighs an accommodation; Mill and Keynes, and Howson as SEP reports him, hold that timing adds nothing once prior grounds are counted. In the reform debate, Devezer et al. deny that preregistration has statistical force, and Wagenmakers affirms it.
- For the method (my inference): keeps: rule 6, on another ground — as a guard against selective reporting (Fisher through Mayo at M-6/6; HARKing at M-6/4). Its sentence on origin and test should be recorded as the repository's choice on a disputed point.

### F1.4 Mill's chapter carries the test condition, but in context his "hypothesis" admits what the method calls opinion, and his "proof" is stronger than "supported"

- Answers: 10, 7; frontier 9
- Evidence:
  - Mill 1868, III.xiv §4 — URL as F1.3 — full text — "An hypothesis being a mere supposition, there are no other limits to hypotheses than those of the human imagination"; "It being thus shown that no other hypothesis would accord with the facts, the assumption was proved"; "In all these cases, verification is proof"; an hypothesis of an unknown cause "is only useful by suggesting a line of investigation which may possibly terminate in obtaining real proof"
  - SEP "Prediction versus Accommodation" — as F1.3 — §1 quotes the 1843 wording "impress the ignorant vulgar" and "by scientific thinkers", where the 1868 text has "the uninformed" and "by persons of scientific attainments"
- Standing: one source, with the edition variant seen in a second
- For the method (my inference): the text says otherwise — Mill defines a hypothesis by insufficient evidence, not by a named refuting outcome. His proof excludes every alternative (the Method of Difference) and yields an "inductive truth", where rule 7 leaves every standing open.

### F1.5 The "National Academy of Sciences" definitions are a course page's adaptation, and its hypothesis and law contradict rule 7 and the table of standings

### F1.6 Rule 4 stands between two cited texts that disagree, Hardwig on trust and the Royal Society on verification, and it names neither the tension nor its choice

- Answers: 6, 8; frontier 9
- Evidence:
  - Hardwig in SEP "Social Dimensions of Scientific Knowledge" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/scientific-knowledge-social/ — full text — §2, cited at M-4/7 — members "are not in a position to evaluate the results of other members’ work, and hence, all must take one anothers’ results on trust"
  - Royal Society history page — https://royalsociety.org/about-us/who-we-are/history/ — full text — on the motto, cited at M-4/5–6 — "to verify all statements by an appeal to facts determined by experiment"
- Standing: two or more independent sources (they disagree with each other)
- For the method (my inference): the text says otherwise — Hardwig denies what a reader who cannot evaluate a test would need for "The support comes from the test itself, whoever its author". Accepting a test "without repeating it once the source is opened" falls short of the motto. Rule 4 should be named as a decided compromise between the two.

### F1.7 Popper makes the acceptance of a test's basic statements a decision; the method's "fact" leaves this out

- Answers: 17, 16; frontier 9
- Evidence:
  - SEP "Karl Popper" — https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/ — full text — the section on criticisms, quoting LSD 2002: 91–92 — "Thus it is decisions which settle the fate of theories."; basic statements "are, from the logical point of view, accepted by an act, by a free decision"; "there can be little doubt but that it constitutes a form of conventionalism in its own right"
  - Popper, LSD selections in Miller (ed.), *Popper Selections*, pp. 133-161 — https://earthweb.ess.washington.edu/roe/Knowability_590/Week1/Popper.doc — full text, with scanning errors — "These decisions will of i course depend in their turn upon the aim which we choose from among a number of possible aims"
- Standing: two or more independent sources
- For the method (my inference): missing — M-0, which treats the rules as a decision serving an aim, is faithful to Popper. "A fact is the material of a test: an observation anyone can repeat or open" omits that someone decides to read a run's outcome as a pass or a fail.
