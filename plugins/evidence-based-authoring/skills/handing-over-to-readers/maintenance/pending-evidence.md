# Pending evidence of the reading contract

The rules and clauses that no opened `direct` or `transfer` source backs, in three groups: those held out of the reference files — a whole rule leaves a numbering gap — those that ship on the committed eval run alone as `practice`, and the clauses of `references/explaining.md`, `references/explainer.md`, `references/media.md`, `references/page.md` and rule 18 that ship on the operator's readings alone. Each entry names what the backing search found and did not find and what would admit it; the research note `research/2026-10-21_reading-contract-evidence.md` § Rules and data holds the search result per rule, `research/2026-11-07_report-forms-evidence.md` § Rules and data per clause, and `research/2026-11-14_explanation-page-evidence.md` § Rules and data for the explanation page. A script carries no check for an entry of the first group.

## Held out of the reference files

- **Rule 10, separate passes.** Text: "Each filter is applied to the whole list as its own pass." Found: nothing opened — Basili et al. 1996, doi:10.1007/BF00368702, gave metadata only; Ciolkowski 2009 was rate-limited, and its relay aggregate says perspective-based reading does not beat a checklist; the lead DOI for Porter, Votta & Basili 1995 resolves to another paper. Admits it: Basili et al. 1996 and Ciolkowski 2009 opened, and the correct DOI of Porter, Votta & Basili 1995. The definitional sentence of rule 10 ships, and `SKILL.md` step 4 runs rules 3–9 over the list without claiming that separate passes find more.
- **Rule 17, the reason clause.** Text: "The decisive reason stands inside the question, not as a separate justification beside it." Found: none. Admits it: a reading study comparing a decision question with the reason built into it against the same question followed by a separate justification.
- **Rule 23, the heading by content.** Text: "The heading names its content." Found: the `D-01` card says nothing about headings; Watanabe et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.17084, measures structure density, not heading wording (weak). Admits it: a rule card that covers heading wording, or an opened reader study of it.
- **Rule 26.** Text: "Every instruction of the list's owner is recorded with its quote, so that the rules can be rebuilt after the session." Found: Falessi et al. 2013, doi:10.1145/2491509.2491515, is weak and does not test quoting; the lead Mäder & Egyed 2015, doi:10.1007/s10664-014-9314-z, is not opened. Admits it: Mäder & Egyed 2015 opened, or one controlled study showing that traces from the work back to the instruction's source improve later work.
- **Rule 27.** Text: "After every series of edits the list is run through checks 3–9 again: items that passed the first run fall on the second." Found: nothing opened; Biffl, Freimut & Laitenberger 2001 is relay (a reinspection's net gain is positive but lower than the first inspection's). Admits it: one opened reinspection study — Biffl et al. 2001, or Biffl & Gutjahr 2002 in Empirical Software Engineering. `SKILL.md` step 6 keeps the run of the check scripts on the final text, which rests on the scripts and on eval client-question-list E1, operator-report-with-failing-build E1 and question-to-operator-with-default E1.
- **No narrative of the work, no retelling of the diff.** Text: "Narrative of the work and a retelling of what the diff shows are left out: the reader cannot check the first and sees the second himself." Found: no source compares a narration of the steps with a statement of the result; Pirouzkhah et al. 2026, arXiv:2602.14611, bounds the diff clause the other way — PRs whose description explains the code changes are 12–20 % more likely to be merged. Admits it: a reader study comparing a description that narrates the steps or restates the diff with one that states the result, the purpose and the evidence, measured on reviewer accuracy or time. The shipped text states what a report carries, and `scripts/check_report.py` holds no narrative check.
- **Explaining, two research figures.** Text: "a causal connective raises comprehension of [the relation between neighbouring facts]" under the clause on cause and effect, and "names and roles of the parts given before the causal account raise understanding (d = 0.75)" under the clause on introducing a thing before its use. Found: no opened source is traced for either; the clauses ship on the operator's readings (line "Explaining: parts under headings, cause before effect, a thing introduced before its use, plain words" of `maintenance/rules-provenance.md`). The d = 0.75 matches the pre-training principle of the multimedia-learning handbook chapter on essential processing (Mayer & Pilegard 2014), which I have not opened. Admits them: each source opened, with its locator, in the 2026-11-07 note § What the literature adds.
- **A calculator as a picture.** Text: "the result is computed by a formula: a calculator, the inputs in the reader's words, the cost among the outputs". Found: one explanation page carried one; its formula was written a second time beside the code, and the build did not compare the two (2026-11-14 note § Rules and data). Admits it: a build step that checks the page's formula against the code's results on given inputs, or the same picture as a table of computed cases.
- **Page scripts of the author's own.** Text: options that add a script, a style sheet and data to a page, with a contract of what a script may rely on in the template. Found: the operator's decision at Reading 7 that figures are drawn from ready parts; the one page that carried its own scripts had a script error seen only in a browser; a style element already passes the body. Admits it: a second page whose figure no ready part of the template can draw.
- **A second language version.** Text: one builder for both languages, a check that two bodies keep the same markup, a translator who returns the places where the source is wrong. Found: one page in two languages. Admits it: a second page delivered in two languages.
- **A cold read part by part.** Text: "the reader gets one part, the table of terms and the questions that point to that part". Found: never run; one agent run a part. Admits it: one explanation read both ways, with what the part-by-part read found beyond the whole read.
- **The chart of a recorded run as a ready part.** Text: panels, stage numbers, the value under the cursor. Found: 253 lines written for the data of one page. Admits it: a second mechanism drawn with the same part.

- **Explaining, the twenty-word limit as a threshold.** Text: "under about twenty words"; `scripts/check_report.py` warns past twenty. Found: the narration the operator approved peaks at 16 words a sentence and the written stories he found tangled reach 40 (2026-11-07 note § Measurements); Cardosi 1993 and Prinzo et al. 2009 bound the items per spoken message, not the words. Admits it: a reading or listening study that varies sentence length in technical prose and measures comprehension or retelling, giving the length where it drops.
- **Explaining, the three-sentence bound of the opening.** Text: "in three to five sentences"; the check warns below three. Found: the operator found an opening of two short sentences too thin (§ The readings, Reading 6); a report written under the rule opened with five. Admits it: a study varying the length of the summary paragraph before a technical text, measured on the reader's retelling or on his decision.
- **Media, the same text beside a figure while the reader starts the sound.** Text: "The page's prose is the story as it is spoken, sentence for sentence", beside the figures. Found: with graphics, identical on-screen text hurts in system-paced lessons (Mayer & Fiorella 2014, README 43, median d = 0.86); without graphics it helps (Moreno & Mayer 2002, README 40). Admits it: a study of learner-paced narration with its identical text beside a diagram, against the narration without the text, measured on comprehension.
- **The story form for a client reader.** Text: the rules of `references/explaining.md` and `references/media.md` are written for any deliverable.
