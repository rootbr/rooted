# Research note — Security hygiene in ordinary code

Topic `security-hygiene-in-ordinary-code` of group J. Input and security hygiene, domain `input`, id prefix `INP`. Formulation locators: PP 'Stay Safe Out There' (The Pragmatic Programmer, 20th anniversary ed., ch. 7 'While You Are Coding': minimize attack surface, least privilege, secure defaults, encrypt sensitive data, maintain security updates); SWEBOK ch. 3 §3.7 (SWEBOK Guide V4.0 ch. 3 'Software Design' §3.7; its V3 counterpart is ch. 2 §2.7 'Security' under 'Key Issues in Software Design', transcribed at ligurio/swebok-v3 master 2_software_design.md).

The canon puts security hygiene inside ordinary coding, not only inside security features. The Pragmatic Programmer 2019 Topic 43 'Stay Safe Out There' (unfetched, paraphrased) says to minimize the attack surface, distrust external input, ask for least privilege, choose secure defaults, keep secrets out of version control and never hand-roll crypto. SWEBOK V3 §3.7 (fetched; the V4 ch. 3 §3.7 locator could not be opened) says construction faults outside security functionality cause vulnerabilities. Code Complete ch. 8 and Building Secure and Reliable Systems ch. 5, 6 and 12 give the trust-boundary barricade, safe-by-construction sinks and contextual autoescaping. The openable standards layer (OWASP cheat sheets, ASVS 5.0, the OWASP SCP checklist and SEI CERT rules) states each hygiene rule. It also resolves the canon's main dispute: PP's 'sanitize input before the database or view' is not the injection defence. Binding at the query and contextual encoding at the output are, and validation at entry is a separate allow-list-and-reject check. Practitioners disagree on several points. Where secrets live (twelve-factor, Ruff S105 and Sonar S2068 say environment variables; OWASP Secrets Management and Docker advise against them) is yielded to runtime-configuration. Whether an argument vector is enough is answered no by argument injection (OWASP, CERT IDS07-J) and by Windows batch files (CPython docs, Rust CVE-2024-24576). Secure defaults are weighed against backward compatibility: PEP 476 and Go 1.19 chose secure defaults, while PEP 506 and Jinja kept the compatible one. OWASP requires server-side binding where psycopg uses driver-side composition. CPython rejects Ruff S307's literal_eval remedy for untrusted data. Ruff S311 flags every non-cryptographic random call, while PEP 506 and the OWASP Cryptographic Storage sheet allow such generators for non-security randomness. The AI-assistant studies disagree (Pearce 2022 and Perry 2023 against Asare 2022). Research supports the rules with measurements. Meli et al. 2019 found secrets leaked in over 100,000 repositories with a median 20 s to discovery, Rahman et al. 2019 a 20-month median lifetime, and Feng et al. 2022 password leaks in over 60,000 repositories. Egele et al. 2013 and Georgiev et al. 2012 tie misuse to insecure or overridden defaults, Lazar et al. 2014 found 83% of crypto CVEs were library misuse, and Felt et al. 2011 found a third of Android apps over-privileged. Acar 2016, Fischer 2017 and Chen 2019 show that insecure forum snippets reach production. Static-analysis tools in all five languages check the rules mechanically but mostly at low confidence, below Software Engineering at Google's 10% false-positive bar. The cards are therefore reviewer-judged, with tools named in Limits. The topic yields 12 cards. Threat modelling, authentication and authorization schemes, and cryptographic and protocol design remain reserved specialties that the cards name and stop at.

## Formulation layer

I opened the SWEBOK V3 chapters through ligurio/swebok-v3: design §2.7, §5.2, §6.2 and §7.5; construction §1.5, §3.2, §3.3, §3.6, §3.7 and §4.4; testing §2.2.7 and §3.2.4. I also opened all 97 Things essays, the aposd-vs-clean-code discussion, which says nothing about security, and Software Engineering at Google. Building Secure and Reliable Systems ch. 5, 6 and 12 are openable under CC BY 4.0 at google/building-secure-and-reliable-systems, and they are the strongest canon statement of safe-by-construction APIs, typed injection sinks, contextual autoescaping and crypto libraries that refuse raw keys. The Google style guides and the OWASP developer documents (DevGuide, Proactive Controls 2024, the Secure Coding Practices checklist, and the Secure Product Design and Attack Surface Analysis cheat sheets) were also fetched and give openable wording for each rule this topic owns. PP Topic 43 'Stay Safe Out There', SWEBOK V4, Code Complete ch. 8, Saltzer and Schroeder, Writing Secure Code, Secure by Design, Effective Java Item 85 and the CERT Top 10 could not be opened and are paraphrased from memory, so none of them can be a card's only anchor. The canon disagrees on four points. First, where secrets live: PP and twelve-factor say config files or environment variables, Proactive Controls C2 says not in code, config files or environment variables, and the SCP checklist says an encrypted config file. Second, whether sanitizing input defends against injection: PP says sanitize input, while OWASP C3 and Secure Product Design say input validation does not replace parameterization or encoding. Third, 97 Things' 'put all assets under version control' against the no-secrets-in-VCS rule. Fourth, Google's preference for compile-time type checks over analysis tuned for false-positive rates, which BSRS itself says contrasts with the research literature.

## Reception layer

Static-analysis tools in all five languages check this topic's hygiene mechanically: Ruff and Bandit, SonarSource, gosec, SpotBugs and find-sec-bugs, ESLint and typescript-eslint, Semgrep and Mozilla's no-unsanitized. Most of these rules run at low confidence and warn that they produce false positives. The secret and shell rules are the noisiest, and sonar-python has closed its untainted shell rule (S4721) and eval rule (S1523), while its untainted formatted-SQL rule (S2077) stays in the default profile. Six positions are contested. (1) 'Sanitize your inputs' against parameterizing or encoding at the sink: OWASP, ASVS and Andy Lester take the second side, while xkcd 327 and the messages of Bandit B608, Ruff S602 and Semgrep still say 'sanitize'. (2) Environment variables as the store for secrets: the 12-factor app, Ruff S105 and Sonar S2068 recommend them, and OWASP Secrets Management and Docker advise against them. (3) Whether an argument vector is enough against command injection: it is not, as shown by argument injection, Rust CVE-2024-24576 and Python's advice to use shell=True when launching Windows batch files. (4) Secure defaults against backward compatibility: Jinja ships with autoescape off, PEP 506 rejected a secure-by-default random, and the Go team argued over PATH lookup. (5) Server-side against client-side binding: OWASP against psycopg. (6) Validation, both Postel-tolerant and as a boolean check, against parsing at the boundary: RFC 9413 and Alexis King. The WebSearch budget was spent, so the following were not opened: Hacker News and Lobsters threads, blogs hosted off GitHub (Ned Batchelder, Angus Croll), the 2024 CISA Secure by Design alerts, and vendor secret-sprawl reports. The freshness check rests on OWASP Top 10:2025 and on the current rewrite of the OWASP Input Validation cheat sheet.

## Evidence layer

I found evidence for nine candidate rules. They cover allow-list validation at the trust boundary, the driver's parameter binding, argument vectors without a shell, contextual output encoding, no evaluation of untrusted input, no secrets in code or history, least privilege, secure defaults, and vetted library primitives. Every rule has an openable standard (OWASP cheat sheets, ASVS 5.0 items, SEI CERT rules from the cmu-sei GitHub copy, SWEBOK V3), and most have official documentation in several languages (Python, Go, Rust, Node/MDN/React, JDK) plus static-analysis rule pages (Ruff, SonarPython, gosec, SpotBugs, ESLint, Scorecard). WebSearch was not available, so I reached papers only through abstracts hosted on GitHub: the neverworkintheory bib files, conference abstract datasets (gled0n S&P/CCS CSVs, a DBLP ICSE dump, an NDSS RIS file) and archived full texts. That way I fetched Acar 2016/2017, Fischer 2017, Felt 2011, Egele 2013, Georgiev 2012, Lazar 2014, Nadi 2016, Meng 2018, Chen 2019, Meli 2019, Rahman 2019, Basak 2022/2023, Feng 2022 and the AI-assistant studies, the last through secondary quotations in an OpenSSF guide. Four searches found no openable abstract: Scholte 2012 on type-based validation, Weinberger 2011 on XSS sanitization, Richards 2011 on eval, and Saltzer and Schroeder 1975. These are recorded as unfetched. The sources disagree in four places. Ruff S307 recommends ast.literal_eval for input, while the CPython docs advise against calling it on untrusted data. Ruff and Sonar point secrets to environment variables, while OWASP Secrets Management does not recommend them unless no other method is possible. OWASP says validation and escaping do not replace parameterization. Asare 2022 finds Copilot no worse than humans, against Pearce 2022 and Perry 2023. Georgiev 2012 adds a nuance: even safe defaults can be overridden by mistake. The two hands-on runs (sqlite3/subprocess and Go html/template) were executed locally and are not openable until they are committed as fixtures.

## Spine — candidate rules in source order

1. **A value that enters across a trust boundary is checked on the server where it enters against allow-list rules for its type, format, length and range, and is rejected when it does not match** — Every value that crosses into the component from outside its trust boundary (a request, file, message, partner feed, internal API or stored record) is checked server-side at the point of entry against field-specific allow-list rules for type, format, length and range, and is rejected rather than cleaned or stripped when it fails; client-side checks are feedback only. ASVS 2.2.1 scopes the requirement at Level 1 to input used for business or security decisions and at Level 2 and up to all input. Passing validation does not make a value safe for a query, command or page (parameterize-queries, contextual-output-encoding); preconditions of routines inside the boundary belong to defensive-programming (ERR-13), and the structure of input and the choice of parser to parsing-and-grammar-based-input.
