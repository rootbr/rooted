# Reading contract — evidence report

The research behind `handing-over-to-readers` finds that the load of reading generated text falls through three levers that act before reading: moving the check from the text to evidence the agent could not forge, a scope boundary set before generation, and the decision not to generate for a class of tasks where editing accepted output costs more than writing it, the last lever resting on one source. Wherever no independent oracle exists, reading stays, rebuilt — by risk, with the expectation written down first and with claims checked against their evidence — because reading fluent prose finds a plausible error unreliably. The research report (Russian, in the operator's research project outside this repository) went through a critical appraisal, which found its first main conclusion unsupported, weakened all seven theses and refuted none; the conclusion above is the narrowed one and holds with medium confidence: evidence produced and checked outside the agent's write permissions where an oracle with measured coverage of the defect class exists, a scope boundary before generation, and rebuilt reading where no oracle exists.

The skill takes four things from the research:

- a first page written as a shift handover (risk, the reader's action, what is not done, the condition under which the conclusion is wrong) with a status line copied from exit codes;
- every claim about code anchored at `path:line` with the fragment of that line;
- questions passed through the operator's filters and delivered as decision cards with a consequence in every option;
- reversible decisions taken by default into a registry.

Of the skill's 27 rules, 16 ship on an opened source, six on the committed eval run alone, three point at a rule card or define a term, and two wait whole for evidence. [Rules and data](#rules-and-data) is the entry point for a rule of the skill. [Sources](#sources) holds 129 entries for 128 distinct sources: 46 seen first-hand, 43 whose locator and title alone were verified, and 40 relayed. This note is compiled from the report and its seven findings files; the source classification and the backing search made for it are folded into [Sources](#sources) and [Rules and data](#rules-and-data).

## What was investigated and why

The operator, a senior Java developer working through Claude Code, finds that proofreading its output has become the bottleneck of his work: specifications, the assistant's questions, code, comments and agents' reports. The research asked how that reading load is lowered on the reader's side, in the production of volume and in the process. Its subject is generated text nobody wanted to read, the Russian coinage rendered here as "neuroslop"; sentence-level style is outside it and stays with the `S-` and `D-` rule cards of `/auditing-ai-context`.

Beside the literature the research holds one object: 27 rules the operator wrote for a list of questions to an addressee — a client, a neighbouring team, a product owner. They cover where candidates come from (rules 1–2), the filters a candidate passes in order (3–10), how an item is written (11–21), order and form (22–24), and the record (25–27). The skill generalises them to any addressee, the operator of the session included: the addressee's profile changes only what he can open himself and the format he receives, and the rules stay the same. The skill's `references/rules.md` carries them under their numbers.

Ten methods built the question map: nine produced 352 questions, and a coverage audit added 20 to close gaps. Deduplication left 183 questions in 12 clusters, beside 10 frontier questions and 7 zones of open terrain. Seven research blocks took the clusters in parallel:

- A — the reader: the problem itself, attention, trust and limits;
- B — production: where volume comes from and how to set it before generation; questions and decisions;
- C — form and channels of delivery;
- D — artifacts one by one: specifications, code, comments, tests;
- E — checking by risk-based sample, and trust without reading;
- F — process, roles and the economics of attention;
- G — long-term cost, hidden pipeline failures and deliberate attacks.

## Method

1. **Question map** — the ten methods above.
2. **Seven blocks in parallel**, each with a budget of 30 searches and 20 fetches. A block had to open a source before asserting it and had to label every conclusion with one of four confidence labels: "confirmed by 2+ sources", "one source", "practitioner opinion", "no data". Six blocks could not write their findings file under the harness's write protection; the orchestrator wrote their texts from the returned answers without editing, and block D wrote its own file. The Claude Code documentation was read for version 2.1.2xx with the Opus 5 and Opus 5.5 models.
3. **The compiled report**: a main conclusion with seven theses, the technique map, twelve sections by cluster, the figures known only from secondary sources, the open questions and 77 numbered load-bearing sources; the full source lists, about 250 entries, close each findings file.
4. **Critical appraisal** by `/appraising-research`: seven critique methods in parallel, adjudication, six debates and a synthesis.
5. **Rewrite of the report** by the appraisal, which set the main conclusion and the theses to the form [The seven theses](#the-seven-theses) gives, with each figure checked against its source (Khan, Iqbal & Horvitz, Fregnan, Renze & Guven, Giskard, Advani); the list of evidence that the skill's `references/evidence.md` carries (the grading code and the build and CI configuration outside the agent's write zone, the read log from the harness transcript, the check of an anchor's fragment against its claim); races, inter-thread order and p99 as a class a green test does not catch; a must-read class by cost of loss; and eleven further sources.
6. **The skill and its eval**: the `handing-over-to-readers` skill turns the result into a contract for every deliverable to a human, and three evals ran with and without it ([The eval as hands-on evidence](#the-eval-as-hands-on-evidence)).

The locators and verdicts of this note stand in two of its sections. [Sources](#sources) classifies every source of the report and resolves its locator. [Rules and data](#rules-and-data) holds, per rule, the research a search for the claim behind it found within a budget, with the fit of each source — `direct` (the claim's own setting), `transfer` (another setting with the same mechanism) or `weak` — and the disposition of the rule.

**First-hand and relay.** This note counts a figure only where the report's blocks or a worker of this transfer saw its source. [Sources](#sources) marks each entry with one of three statuses:

- `first-hand` — the text (abstract, HTML or PDF) was seen: by the backing search, by the author of this note, or by the output-style programme, whose note `research/2026-08-02_output-style-evidence.md` lists the source as verified first-hand (most such sources are entries of `README.md` §References);
- `title` — the sources registry resolved the locator and saw the title; nobody read the text for this note, so the figures attached are the report's;
- `relay` — not opened here.

Only a `first-hand` source carries a rule of the skill. A figure that reaches this note only through the report's prose is attributed to the report. A vendor or practitioner source carries the label "formulation only" and backs no rule.

**The appraisal in numbers.** It produced 122 raw findings: 67 confirmed (54 defects and 13 strengths), 51 merged into them, 3 rejected at adjudication because the report already stated the caveat, and 1 refuted in debate (ACH6: Buçinca et al. did measure accuracy on wrong advice). The debates confirmed RED2, RED7 and ACH5 with high confidence and ACH7 and ASM3 with medium. The main conclusion in its first form was unsupported (LOG1, critical): its claim that both levers were measured better than any reading technique contradicted the report's own labels ("no data for measurements of Claude Code's levers", "order by mechanism, without a measurement"), and the reading techniques it was compared with were outside the research. In its narrowed form it holds with medium confidence, and all seven theses are weakened with none refuted. The sources are not invented and most figures are quoted correctly; in 14 findings a caveat stood in a findings file or in the report's body and dropped out when the main conclusion or the technique map was compiled. Three checks stay undone: the levers before generation against classic inspection techniques on one material; robustness to a false premise under the Concise style (LOG3); and the share of default decisions the operator overturns, measured on his history (ASM17).

**Limits the report names.** The report transfers laboratory constants — vigilance decrement, the prevalence effect, split attention — to proofreading by mechanism, without a measurement on it, and transfers the data from radiology, audit, e-discovery and aviation the same way. The research serves one operator. Almost all figures are more than a year old and come from models of 2022–2025. The vendors among the sources (Faros, Sonar, CodeRabbit, BetterUp, SmartBear) sell the remedy for the problem they measure. The data hold no controlled measurement of proofreading accuracy on an agentic diff, no volume threshold beyond which sampling is unavoidable, no measurement of Claude Code's mechanisms on visible volume, and no multi-year trajectory of a developer who reads instead of writing. The report's target quantity is missed defects per minute of the operator: some techniques (own judgement before the machine's answer, must-read classes, debate between models, a second pass over comments) lower misses at the price of minutes, so a self-measurement counts the operator's total time as well.

**Where this note departs from the report.** Where the backing search read a source the report relays, the note carries the source's figure:

- read-back: the report's 37 % of erroneous read-backs missed is a search-snippet figure; Cardosi 1993 [85], read first-hand, gives 11 % (three of 27 read-back errors);
- debate: the lone-defence comparison is consultancy (78 % with human judges, 54 % with LLM judges), not the naive judges (60 %, 48 %) [39];
- the finding that a convincing wrong explanation raises trust belongs to Si et al. 2023 [40] (accuracy 0.35 with the wrong explanation alone against 0.54 with the retrieved evidence); Vasconcelos et al. [41] study uncertainty highlighting in code completion;
- the false-success shares (45–48 %, 75.8 %) and the judges' AUROC belong to Advani [42], and the unread-file shares (67.9 %, 80.4 %) to OverclaimBench [43];
- the "subtask boundary" result of Adamczyk & Bailey [18] stays relay: the backing search could not open the paper.

## The seven theses

Each thesis is given in the report's narrowed form, with the report's confidence label and the appraisal's revised confidence for the conclusion it narrows.

1. **Volume is laid down by training and by the reader's own instructions, and it is cut cheapest before generation.** The load has two parts with different levers. Reading filler shrinks with the report form in the agent's definition, the Concise style and a Stop hook; checking claims shrinks only with a scope boundary in the task statement, because that boundary cuts the number of objects to check (Baum, Schneider & Bacchelli 2019 [69], doi:10.1007/s10664-018-9676-8). Reward models reward length (Singhal et al. [9], arXiv:2310.03716). "Write shorter" cuts the load-bearing with the rest: in Renze & Guven [11] (arXiv:2401.05618) the average effect of brevity on accuracy is negligible, the exception being GPT-3.5 on maths with a drop of 27.69 %; Giskard [12], a vendor, reports hallucination resistance falling "in extreme cases up to 20 %"; both measure compressed reasoning, not a compressed report. Below average length a reader detects errors less often (Friedman et al. [68], arXiv:2603.06878: 0.245 against 0.542, n = 24, not code). The order of the levers — task statement, report form in the agent's definition, Concise style of the main session, Stop hook — is set by mechanism, without a measurement. Label: confirmed by 2+ sources for the mechanism, no data for measurements of Claude Code's levers. Appraisal: medium for the origin of length, low for the order of the levers.
2. **An agent's self-report is not evidence.** False success made up 45–48 % of failures in single-control tau2-bench domains and 75.8 % among AppWorld coding-agent trajectories with explicit status claims; an LLM judge reading the same text without access to the environment separates false success at AUROC 0.65 on tau2-bench and 0.54 on AppWorld (Advani 2026 [42], arXiv:2606.09863). Agents left unread the files they were asked to review in 67.9 % of runs and were misleading about it in 80.4 % of those (Smyth et al. 2026 [43], arXiv:2609.20812). Each figure rests on one preprint, and the 45–48 % is a share of failures, not of success claims. Evidence is a trace the agent could not produce: the test, the grading code and the build and CI configuration lie outside the agent's write zone, since otherwise `sys.exit(0)` or a planted `conftest.py` (MacDiarmid et al. 2025 [70], arXiv:2511.18397) or `testFailureIgnore` in a Maven build yield an honest green exit code; the harness takes the exit code; the read log comes from the harness transcript, not from the report; a script checks the fragment at a `path:line` anchor against the claim, not only the existence of the line. A test is evidence only about behaviour it distinguishes deterministically: races, inter-thread order and p99 need their own evidence (jcstress, model checking, JMH against a baseline), and without it reading is mandatory (CONCUR [71], arXiv:2603.03683). Label: confirmed by 2+ sources, both recent preprints. Appraisal: high for "a self-report is not evidence", medium for the list of evidence.
3. **Uniform full reading loses reliability at a low defect rate, and the measured defect rates of LLM output are not low.** At a target prevalence of 1 % misses rise from about 7 % to about 30 % (Wolfe, Horowitz & Kenner 2005 [124], secondary); Fleck & Mitroff [73] (doi:10.1111/j.1467-9280.2007.02006.x) attribute the effect to a shift of the response criterion and remove it by letting the observer correct a response. Vigilance falls within the first 15–30 minutes (Mackworth; Teichner [128], secondary). Machine highlighting lowers sensitivity to what it leaves unmarked (Alberdi et al. 2004 [44], doi:10.1016/j.acra.2004.05.012 — 52 % against 68 % for unprompted cancers). The report's own figures are far from rare: 52 % of answers with errors (Kabir et al. [3], arXiv:2308.02312), 44–45 % of programs with a CWE (Pearce et al. [125]; Veracode [36], vendor), 29.6 % of test-passing patches diverging from the reference, of which about 11 % are incorrect by manual check (Wang, Pradel & Liu [34], arXiv:2503.15223). The sampling frame therefore applies to a class of tasks with a measured low defect rate, not to the whole stream. In place of full reading the report sets deterministic must-read classes by cost of loss (the order path, limits and kill switch, feature flags, deployment configuration; Knight Capital [75] lost about 440 million dollars in 2012 on a reused flag and an incomplete deployment, a secondary figure) and by cost of attack (CI, permissions, dependencies, build, tests, the agent's files), and a sample from the rest, as in audit (ISA 530 [46]) and e-discovery (Grossman & Cormack [47]). One operator will not collect the roughly 400 positive findings that calibrating recall needs, and the count resets when the model changes; an elusion test over the unread part alone overstates recall (ACEDS [74], "Elusion Illusion"), so the sample is drawn from all strata or recall is estimated with seeded defects. Label: confirmed by 2+ sources for the mechanisms, no data for LLM output. Appraisal: medium for deterministic must-read classes, low for sampling with a measurable miss risk.
4. **The human stays in the loop through active judgement — a prediction before the machine's answer and questioning after it — instead of passive reading.** Own judgement before the answer is shown raises the share of the machine's errors rejected (Buçinca, Malaya & Gajos [6], arXiv:2102.09692: correct decisions on wrong advice 0.09 against 0.03) at the price of more rejections of its correct answers, so overall accuracy does not rise (0.33 against 0.35; Fogliato et al. [77], arXiv:2205.09696: 90 % against 91 %); it is not measured on reviewing a diff. Debate between two models gives a judge 88 % (human) and 76 % (LLM) against 78 % and 54 % under consultancy, one model defending one side, whose accuracy falls as its persuasiveness grows; a naive judge reaches 60 % and 48 % (Khan et al. [39], arXiv:2402.06782, with Anthropic co-authors). Comprehension is higher in the group without an assistant (Anthropic [58], vendor, randomised: 67 % against 50 %); its subgroups "asked the model" and "delegated" are observational, n = 2 and n = 4, and Anthropic has a product interest in its Learning and Explanatory modes. Label: one source per technique. Appraisal: medium for own judgement before advice on the machine's errors (one study, not code, strengthened by the refutation of ACH6), low for the opposition "before, not after".
5. **An assistant's question costs tens of minutes, and part of the questions close before they are asked; the size of that part is not measured.** An interruption by a notification cost about 10 minutes on the switch and another 10–15 before the return to focused work (Iqbal & Horvitz 2007 [17], doi:10.1145/1240624.1240730 — 27 users, email and instant-messaging alerts, not an agent's questions). Of Claude Code's stops, 35 % are a choice between approaches and 21 % gathering diagnostics (Anthropic [15], vendor telemetry whose clusters the vendor's model labelled). The operator's rules 3–7 (is the answer known? does it change the work? is the gap ours? is it reversible? is it their decision?) are exactly the filters the `AskUserQuestion` schema lacks. The transfer of an alert's cost to an agent's question is a hypothesis. Label: confirmed by 2+ sources for the cost of interruption. Appraisal: low.
6. **The form of delivery decides what gets checked at all.** The odds of finding a defect in the file shown last are 64 % lower — an odds ratio for one of two seeded defects (Fregnan et al. 2022 [21], arXiv:2208.04259). A claim and its evidence in one field of view remove split attention (d = 0.85 on complex material; Ginns 2006 [106], secondary). Voice suits orientation, not inferential checking (Clinton-Lisell [25], doi:10.3102/00346543211060871 — g = 0.36 in favour of reading). Label: confirmed by 2+ sources. Appraisal: medium; the order effect is causal, its size and generality are overstated.
7. **The report reads the regulator as requiring an accountable human, a testing methodology and a record of approval (RTS 6 Art. 5, DORA RTS Art. 17); project norms differ (the Linux kernel requires a review of all AI-generated code), and actual practice is already selective.** Of merged agentic PRs, 79.1 % carry no observable feedback loop (Peralta et al. [50], arXiv:2605.22534), an absence of trace rather than proof that nobody read them; 48 % of developers always check AI code (Sonar [51], vendor). None of the norms the report cites shares responsibility with the model: the Linux kernel policy [52], and RTS 6 [53] and the DORA RTS [53a] as the report reads them. I did not open RTS 6 or the DORA RTS: the sources registry records both fetches as failed, so the content of their articles is the report's. Label: one source per figure, confirmed by 2+ sources for the norms. Appraisal: low for the reading of project norms, high for binding approval to a hash.

## Rules and data

The rules were written for questions to a client, and the addressee of an agent's question is the operator; they transfer with one amendment, according to the report: filters 3–7 are run by a model that tells an incomplete task from a complete one poorly (Ambig-SWE [16]), so the share of defaults the operator overturns has to be measured. The table puts each rule beside what the report found and beside what the backing search found afterwards; the last column is the disposition:

- `research` — the rule's source line cites an opened study with its fit;
- `standard` — the source line cites a standard;
- `practice` — the rule rests on the operator's practice, confirmed by the committed eval run; the eval expectation is written `eval/E` (`1/E3` is expectation 3 of eval 1), and the skill's `maintenance/pending-evidence.md` § Shipped on the eval run alone names, for each rule it lists, the study that would back it;

- `card` — the clause applies an existing rule card of `/auditing-ai-context`, and the rules file keeps one pointer line to it.

### What the data add to the rules, and the rules to the data

For an irreversible decision the answer comes as typed input of the consequence, not as a button, and the agent repeats the chosen consequence before acting — a transfer of the read-back, untested on LLM decision cards. Questions accumulate until the boundary of a task, and the Agent SDK offers a deferred question through `defer` in a PreToolUse hook [14]. The "default" card the operator may overturn coincides with rule 6 and with the review-by-board mode of his instructions [127]. Decision fatigue as a bottleneck is disputed (the "hungry judge" effect is contested) while the cost of interruption is robust, so the working lever is fewer decisions through defaults and batching, not less text per decision.

The data found refute no rule. Rule 7 (their decision or ours) and rule 20 (options of a cancelled scheme leave with it) appear in no source the report found; the backing search rests rule 7 on rule 4's expected-value criterion and rule 20 on card D-01. Rule 2 sets the minimal unit of a question the way block E sets the minimal unit of a check: a claim whose referent changes the decision.

## The eval as hands-on evidence

The skill's eval holds three cases, each run once with the skill and once without it. The run was made with Russian prompts and fixtures; the prompts, fixtures and outputs committed with the skill are their English translation, and the grader re-run on the translation reproduces every verdict.

## Open questions

- A controlled measurement of proofreading accuracy on an agentic diff, and of hours-long daily proofreading by an experienced developer: not found; the nearest are METR's RCT and short laboratory tasks (Mozannar, Kabir, Buçinca).
- The share of removable load in LLM output, and the share of volume the reader's own instructions set: only components and a local count.
- The effect of effort, output styles, hooks, subagent definitions and plan mode on visible volume: no measurement; the report sketches a week-long self-measurement (a non-blocking Stop hook counting words, alternating Default and Concise).
- The payback of the loop "reader's edit → generation rule", tested only on a simulated user (CIPHER), and the shelf life of reading techniques after a model change.
- Whether turning a report into decision cards lowers reading load; the effect of the card format with a consequence in each option; challenge-response checklists after Degani & Wiener.
- A comparison of carriers (terminal, file, board, IDE diff); a numeric threshold for the expertise-reversal effect; the effect of a collapsed layer on misses; video explanations of a diff; a detector of "flow or exploration".
- What replaces comment review where comments are banned; defect rates of LLM code on `equals`/`hashCode`, money arithmetic, boundaries and the JMM; ImpossibleBench's read-only-tests mode; the value of Hermans's techniques on LLM code.
- The position of errors inside long output; the volume threshold for switching to sampling; whether attention matches the distribution of defects; when property-based tests, contracts and formal specification replace reading; the number of verification layers at which load grows; a comparison of verification-quality metrics.
- A mechanism to mark what the author has read; the owner of a shared library under agentic changes; a duty to disclose the review method to a client; how the assistant learns the price of the reader's minute; the senior developer's identity as "the one who understands the code".
- The number of agents and the share of generated code at which the role changes; the threshold ratio of inflow to proofreading speed; a hook filter that damaged code blocks; the consistency of the voice and screen channels.
- The levers before generation against classic inspection (Fagan, Kemerer & Paulk, McIntosh et al.) on one material: not done.
- Primary sources beyond the budget: Fagan 1976, Kemerer & Paulk 2009, HTER, Krings 2001, NASA-TLX, IEEE 1028-2008, the texts of ISO 2859-1 and ISA 240, Degani & Wiener 1990, Bainbridge 1983, Shaw & Nave 2026.
