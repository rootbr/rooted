# Research note — Dependency management

Topic `dependency-management` of group H. Tools and hygiene, domain `tooling`, id prefix `TOOL`. Formulation locators: SW2.4 (SWEBOK Guide V4.0 ch. 4 'Software Construction' §2.4; the V3 transcription's §2 'Managing Construction' ends at §2.3 and carries no counterpart, so the V4 text, which is not openable, is the only formulation); SEG21 (Software Engineering at Google ch. 21 'Dependency Management': conflicting requirements and diamond dependencies, importing a dependency, compatibility promises, semantic versioning and its limits, bundled distribution, Live at Head).

The canon agrees that a dependency costs more than its import line. Its formulation is SWEBOK V4 §2.4, known here only as an unfetched paraphrase, and SEG ch. 21, read in full together with ch. 18 from the same book. A dependency brings a network of transitive packages and forces upgrades nobody planned. Its versions belong under version control and are changed deliberately, and code declares directly what it uses. The industry disagrees in five places. (1) Reuse or avoidance of small dependencies: SEG21's 'you should use it' against the Go proverb and the Go blog's 'small dependency tree'. (2) How far SemVer ranges can be trusted: the spec against SEG21's 'lossy estimate', with Raemaekers, Ochoa and Venturini disagreeing on how often non-major releases break. (3) Live at Head against pinned, deterministic builds. (4) Whether a library commits its lock file: Cargo reversed its guidance in 2023 and Poetry leaves it open. (5) Whether every advisory requires an upgrade: OWASP and Scorecard against Abramov's false-positive critique and the reachability studies. The evidence supports eleven cards: (1) declare every directly imported package (Zhang et al. 2026, the Maven docs, the SemVer FAQ, linters in four ecosystems); (2) declare a shipped import as a runtime dependency (Latendresse et al. 2022, deptry); (3) give every requirement a lower bound and no wildcard (Cargo, clippy, uv); (4) use ranges, not pins, in a published library, separated from applications by PEP 440's and Cargo's own condition (PEP 440, PyPA, He et al. 2025); (5) commit the lock file in an application, separated from libraries because a lock never reaches consumers (npm, Cargo, Go, Poetry, uv, PEP 751, Gamage et al. 2026, Venturini et al. 2023); (6) update the lock file with the manifest and (7) install from it in frozen mode (OWASP NPM Security Cheat Sheet, npm ci, Go); (8) remove a declaration whose last use is gone (Soto-Valero et al. 2021); (9) move off a vulnerable version that ships, separated by deployment and reachability (OWASP, ASVS 5.0, Pashchenko 2018, Alfadel 2023, Latendresse 2022, Wu 2023); (10) weigh a new dependency's transitive cost, separated by whether the functionality is trivial or substantial (Zimmermann 2019, Kikas 2017, Abdalkareem 2017 against Massacci 2021); (11) confirm an added package's identity (OWASP NPM Security Cheat Sheet; Spracklen et al., relayed). Two rules stay pending because no source states a separating condition: a cap at the next major version, and a library's lock file. Smallest-step updates have no evidence, as SEG21 itself concedes. Update cadence, SemVer's unreliability and Live at Head have no checkable form on a diff. Duplicate versions and diamond mediation behave differently in each ecosystem, so they yield no cross-language rule.

## Formulation layer

I read SEG21 in full from the CC-licensed Abseil mirror. I also read SEG18 ('Managing Dependencies': strict transitive dependencies, manual versioning, hash manifests, vendoring) and SEG16 (the One-Version rule), plus the authors' later Abseil policy pages. Other canon read in full: the SWEBOK V3 construction and SCM chapters, the relevant 97 Things essays, Twelve-Factor II, and official project formulations (Go proverb and Go blog, the Cargo FAQ before and after the 2023 lock-file change, the Rust blog post announcing that change, PyPA's abstract-vs-concrete requirements, SemVer, the OWASP vulnerable-dependency cheat sheet). Several texts are cited from memory and marked 'paraphrase, unfetched', so none of them can be a card's only anchor. These are SWEBOK V4 §2.4, Russ Cox's 'Our Software Dependency Problem' (the essay SEG21 links), Clean Code ch. 8, Continuous Delivery and The Pragmatic Programmer. The aposd-vs-clean-code discussion has nothing on package dependencies. The canon agrees that a new dependency costs more than its import line (transitive network, forced upgrades), that versions belong pinned in source control with updates made deliberately and tested, and that code should depend directly on what it uses. It disagrees in four places. (1) Reuse versus avoidance: SEG21's 'you should use it' against the Go proverb 'a little copying is better than a little dependency'. (2) SemVer: the spec says it can end dependency hell, while SEG21 calls it a lossy estimate that resolvers treat as absolute. (3) Live at Head versus pinned deterministic builds. (4) Committing lock files for libraries: Cargo reversed its guidance in 2023. The Cargo book source answers 404 on master but is reachable at release tags (rust-lang/cargo/0.99.0/src/doc/src/...).

## Reception layer

WebSearch was spent, so this layer is built from GitHub-hosted pages: package-manager docs for all five ecosystems, linter and analyzer rule pages, two OWASP standards, Go design notes, three practitioner positions with openable sources (Ed Page on the Rust blog, Filippo Valsorda on the Go blog, Dan Abramov on overreacted), and neverworkintheory reviews and transcripts. Quotes collapse source line breaks to spaces and mark cuts with '...'. Five points are argued in the canon. (1) Whether a library commits its lock file: Cargo reversed its 'not for libraries' rule in 2023, Poetry warns that applications ignore a library's lock, and Go has no separate lock file at all (Valsorda, Cox), while Maven has none and Gradle locking is opt-in. (2) How tight version constraints should be: PEP 440 and PyPA discourage exact pins in published libraries, Renovate recommends pinning in applications (disputed by @LinusU, whom it quotes), and Schreiner (not openable) and the Cargo blog argue against upper bounds that are only guesses. (3) Whether to add a dependency at all: Go's 'small dependency tree' culture and SEG's 'nearly free at programming scale' pull in opposite directions. (4) Whether every advisory requires an upgrade: Abramov's 99% false-positive critique and reachability tools such as govulncheck stand against OWASP and Scorecard's 'update to a non-vulnerable version'. (5) How fast to update: Renovate says 'update often', while Dependabot, pnpm, uv and Renovate's own 14-day automerge advice all add a delay before a new release is taken, and Dependabot exempts security updates from it. Declaring every direct dependency and removing unused ones is not contested: Maven docs, eslint-plugin-n (on in its recommended configs), eslint-plugin-import, deptry DEP001–DEP004, knip, go mod tidy, Maven dependency:analyze, cargo-machete/udeps and DepClean all check it, though every tool lists false positives (reflection, generated code, build.rs). Hacker News and Lobsters threads, the 2024–2026 surveys, Henry Schreiner's upper-bound essay, Valsorda's 'Turn Dependabot Off' and Russ Cox's dependency essays could not be opened. They appear only as unfetched items or notes.

## Evidence layer

Each of the six candidate rules has openable support. The rules are: weigh a new dependency against what it brings in transitively, declare every package you import directly, use bounded version constraints, commit the lock file, remove unused declarations, and move off versions with known vulnerabilities. Many papers were opened through GitHub-hosted copies: Soto-Valero's thesis PDF (EMSE 2021, FSE 2021), Scopus and ACM bibliographic exports carrying the abstracts, a sentence-split copy of Zimmermann et al. (USENIX Security 2019), the arXiv RSS archive (lockfile study), an author-hosted Raemaekers PDF and an arXiv-derived copy of Zhang et al. (ASE 2026). Kula et al. and Zerouali et al. are relayed only through the orchestrator's search snippets. Ponta et al. and SWEBOK V4 §2.4 are unfetched, and no copy was found that defines Jafari et al.'s seven dependency smells. The sources disagree on version constraints. PEP 440, the PyPA guide, Cargo and He et al. (FSE 2025) argue against exact pins in a published package's manifest, and Dietrich et al. find developers struggle between predictability and flexibility. Evidence on how reliable semantic versioning is splits three ways: Raemaekers finds about one third of releases break, Ochoa finds 83.4% of upgrades comply, and Venturini finds 44% of manifesting breaks in minor or patch releases. Two rules carry conditions. The lock-file rule is conditional: Cargo and Poetry say a library's lock file does not reach its consumers, and Go uses minimal version selection plus go.sum instead of a lock file. The vulnerability rule is narrowed by deployment and reachability studies (Pashchenko 2018, Latendresse 2022, Wu 2023), and unused-dependency detection is approximate (Maven FAQ, rustc lint, JLBP-22 on reflection).
