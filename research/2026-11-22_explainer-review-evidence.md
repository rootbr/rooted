# An explanation page reviewed by four of its readers — evidence report

The explanation page that the genre of `handing-over-to-readers` was drawn from (`research/2026-11-14_explanation-page-evidence.md`) was reviewed at one meeting by four of its readers. By the summary of that meeting which the owner of this repository gave the session, they could not rebuild the algorithm from the page and went to the code, while at least six of the questions they asked had their answer on the page. The order the genre prescribed stood between them and those answers: the routes, a table of questions and a table of twenty terms after the opening, then parts in the order that introduces each thing before its use, with the rule of the mechanism in the fifth part. The skill takes the three things the readers asked for — an order from the whole to its parts, precision of words, numbers and conditions, and proof by one case — as ten clauses. Each is in force as its owner's decision, and the claim behind each waits as a hypothesis. A search of the literature found 60 published tests and six documents that prescribe a practice; none of the tests ran on an explanation page that an agent wrote for a team of engineers.

Main results:

- Every shortcoming the summary ascribes to the skill's own text stands in that text as it was (§ The shortcomings against the skill's text). What it says of the page and of the meeting was not opened here: the record of the meeting stays with the owner, and the records of the skill carry it as a relay.
- The count the genre named as its own check, the readers' later questions that the text already answers, came out against the genre and moves no standing: no report of the same matter was read beside the page, and no threshold was fixed before the meeting. The reading that can refute the new order is recorded with its threshold: the same readers, the rebuilt page, fewer than six such questions.
- Of the 60 tests, 7 go in the direction of a claim, 3 against one, 6 both ways and 44 answer a neighbouring question. The three that go against come from two sources: one experiment on teaching the parts of a mechanism before the whole, which bears on two claims, and one on beginners who solved problems stated in words more often than the same problems stated as equations.
- Two of the shortcomings are measured elsewhere as failures of documentation: information that is present and not found, and a condition in natural language that practitioners read in two ways.
- No document found prescribes all three things the readers asked of a measurement — the scenario as a file, the command and the spread of the runs — so that clause is a decision with its hypothesis, and the documents stand beside it as accepted practice.
- Fourteen findings of the search bear on `reading-companion`, the skill that walks a reader through a book; by its owner's decision they enter that skill, seven as clauses of its run set and seven as tests beside its claims (§ What bears on the reading companion).

## What was investigated and why

The genre "an explanation for several readers" entered the skill from one page, on the approval of the reader who commissioned it. Its evidence report named the one count that would confirm it: how many of the team's questions after the second version the page already answered (`research/2026-11-14_explanation-page-evidence.md` § Open questions). The review meeting gave that count. Three questions followed. Does the skill's text prescribe what the summary of the meeting says hid the answers? Which of the things the readers asked for become rules, and on what ground does each stand? What have published tests measured about each shortcoming and each remedy?

## Method

**The review.** The owner of the repository gave the session a written summary of the meeting: what the four readers could not do, six of the questions they asked that the page already answered, four failures of precision, the three things the readers asked for, and two points the author of the summary added himself. The session opened neither the page nor the record of the meeting; both stay outside this repository. Every sentence of this note about the page and the meeting relays that summary.

**The skill's text.** The session read the run set of the skill at commit `3d7c6b2` and set each shortcoming the summary ascribes to the skill against the line that prescribes it.

**The clauses.** The session wrote the clauses and, for each, the claim that it works: the measure, what it is compared with, the conditions and the outcome that would refute it, by `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/scientific-method/references/method.md`.

**The literature.** Five researchers searched for tests published to 2026-11-22, one theme each: the order of an explanation; the precision of words, numbers and conditions; proof, blocks and measurement; the first part read before the rest and a check in the builder; and the reported shortcomings as known failures of technical documentation. Four skeptics then opened every source themselves and gave each item a verdict. Of the 67 items, 33 stand as the researcher wrote them and 34 were corrected to what the source says; none was found false, and none stayed unopened. A script fetched the source of each quoted fragment and looked for the words in it: 146 of 152 quotes stand, in a page's text or in the record a service returns for the source, and the six it could not fetch or match are left out of `citations.json`.

**The independent reader.** Each of the twelve new hypotheses was read by a reader that saw nothing of this work and answered the check questions of the seven rules of the method. The kind and the standing held in all twelve. Refutability failed in three: a refuting outcome joined by "and" where the claim promises several things, a part of a claim with no measure of its own, and a measure that counted delivered pages where the claim counts builds. Each was reworded, and each held at a fresh reading. The readers found six places where a record said more than its source or named the wrong place in it, and each is corrected; one source a reader could not open now names its openable copy. One finding stays open, and it is the owner's to settle: six of the twelve first readings fail the rule of severity, and four the rule of reproducibility, for one reason. The `test` field of a hypothesis lists tests of neighbouring questions, none of which compares what the claim compares. The other six readings take those rules as not applicable, since no test of the claim is planned. The records say of each such test that it answers a neighbouring question, and 92 of the 98 hypotheses of the list have this form, so the question is one about the format of a record and not about these twelve.

**Limits.** One page, one meeting, four readers, relayed by one summary. No page built by the new clauses has been read. One researcher searched each theme, with a budget of about sixty tool calls, and 19 leads ended without a test (§ Open questions). Of the 60 tests, 36 were read by their abstract alone. The researchers and the skeptics are models of one family.

## The review

**What the readers could not do.** They could not rebuild the algorithm from the page, and opened the code.

**Questions the page already answered.** At least six: which signals the algorithm has; what the sender reports; over which period the parser's occupancy is counted; whose queue is meant; what the target quantity is made of; of which steps the decision consists.

**What hid the answers.** After the opening the page gave the routes by time, the table of the earlier meeting's questions and a table of about twenty terms; then the parts, ordered so that each thing was introduced before its use; the rule that recomputes the result stood fifth among the parts.

**Four failures of precision.**

- Three terms of one family — the round-trip time, the round-trip time of the path and the minimum round-trip time — were defined in the table of terms through one another.
- The condition "no shorter than 100 ms and two RTT" was retold in words and read in two ways; in the code it is the maximum of the two.
- The word "queue" stood in the main condition and meant several buffers at once.
- One value of a signal was named "socket", while the code sets it when data remains after a frame is assembled.

**What the readers asked for.**

1. An order from the whole to its parts. Right after the opening one part gives the whole mechanism: the signals, the estimates of the model, the rule as pseudocode of a few steps, the state machine as a figure where states switch. It answers a newcomer in his order: which signals, how each is measured, what each gives. Every later part unfolds one line of that level as a small algorithm of its own.
2. Precision. A term appears where it is needed, derived from what is measured and shown on a figure; the table of terms is a reference at the end. A number says what it is a share of, over which period, when it is updated and when it is read. A condition and a calculation are given as a formula; a word has one meaning and a named owner; the value of a signal is defined by the event in the code.
3. Proof. The need for a part is shown by one case, with its starting conditions and its numbers, in which a simpler scheme named by the reader breaks; removing a part from the finished algorithm they did not take as proof. With it: a separate simplified model for each factor, with it and without it; a measurement with its scenario, its command and the spread of its runs; the condition of the model apart from the way of detecting it.

**Their measure.** The text is read without its author and without a model.

**What the author of the summary added.** Two points the readers did not name as a rule: the goal in physical terms and the cause of the complexity stand in the first part, because in the talk these two settled questions.

## The genre's own check

The genre's hypothesis compares an explanation with a report of the same matter and names three measures; the third is the share of the readers' later questions whose answer the text already holds. The meeting gives that count for the explanation alone: at least six. By the method this is an observation that goes against the claim and is no test of it: the comparison the claim names was not made, and the prediction and its threshold were not recorded before the result. It stands in the record of the hypothesis and of the hypothesis on the tables before the first part, each as a relayed source, and both keep the standing `hypothesis`.

What can go against the new order is recorded as the owner's decision on how the page is checked, the line `Explainer: the rebuilt page is read by its four reviewers and by a newcomer` of the provenance map: the same four readers read the rebuilt page, the count is taken from the record of that meeting as the first was, and six or more questions the page already answers, or readers who again cannot retell the algorithm without the code, go against the order. The reading is the owner's check of the page and bears on the order one way only. The readers have read the code since the first review, which by itself predicts fewer such questions, and the page and the records of both meetings stay outside this repository, where no other person can open them. So a count of six or more goes against the order, and a lower count gives the claim on the order no standing above `hypothesis`; the independent reader of the record marked the same two things. The test that could support the order is another one: readers who have not read the code, given the first build or the rebuilt page by lot, with the protocol and the counts kept in the repository. By the owner's decision a lighter reading is taken first, when the occasion comes: the first engineer who joins the team without having read the code retells the algorithm from the rebuilt page alone, and the share of steps he retells is recorded against a threshold of four fifths. It says whether the page reads without the code and nothing on whether its order is the better one. Both readings are fixed in `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/handing-over-to-readers/evals/explainer-reading/protocol.md`.

## What the literature says about each shortcoming

No test below ran on an explanation page for an engineering team. Each sentence names what was measured and on whom; the records in `maintenance/pending-evidence.md` carry the figures with their conditions, and § Sources lists every item.

**An answer the page holds is not found.** This is measured as a failure of documentation. Of 824 documentation-related messages, issues and pull requests coded into 162 kinds of issue, 255 concerned how content is written and organized, and half of the 138 usability issues concerned information that was available and could not be found (Aghajani et al. 2019). Over 30 % of 1,000 Stack Overflow posts on one API named difficulty locating information in the documentation (Fan et al. 2020), and finding information was important to 65 % of 78 practitioners who rated documentation issues (Aghajani et al. 2020). Beside it stands a result that keeps presentation in its place: in two surveys of 323 software professionals at one company, presentation problems drew 25 of 86 problem mentions against 61 for problems of content, and the respondents put five problems of content above every problem of presentation (Uddin & Robillard 2015). No study was found that counts the share of readers' questions a document already answered, the count the genre uses.

**The readers opened the code.** Of 28 professional developers observed at work, 21 took their main knowledge from the source code and its comments and 4 from documentation, which the authors put mainly down to documentation that is missing or out of date (Maalej et al. 2014). Eleven developers given an unfamiliar API and only its documentation spent about 49 % of their time in it, and five of them gave the page of concepts under 6 % of that time (Meng, Steinhardt & Schubert 2019). Neither study compares orders of presentation, so neither says that the page's order sent the readers to the code.

**The rule of the mechanism stood late, after front matter.** The one experiment that bears directly goes against the new order for novices: students given the parts of a mechanism and their states before a narrated animation of the whole did better on transfer in three experiments, against no such training and, in the third, against the same training given after (Mayer, Mathias & Wetzell 2002, by its abstract). The new first part opens with the signals and what each gives before it gives the rule, which is the order that experiment favours inside one part; whether a team reads the whole better before the unfolded parts is what the hypothesis asks. An advance organizer before a text had a small facilitative effect in a meta-analysis of 135 studies (Luiten, Ames & Ackerson 1980), and a label naming the algorithm above a piece of code raised comprehension from 25.77 to 30.12 for 56 readers (Neumüller, Raschke & Tichy 2025). For the table of terms the nearest tests are of glosses for readers of a second language: a gloss at the word served better than one placed elsewhere (AbuSeileek 2011; Marefat, Rezaee & Naserieh 2016), with one study finding the effect of location unclear (Varol & Erçetin 2021).

**The newcomer's questions were not in the registry.** Only neighbouring tests were found. Teachers with more advanced mathematics misjudged what is hard for beginners (Nathan & Petrosino 2003); the pull of one's own knowledge on a judgement of what another person knows is real and small, d = 0.20 over seven experiments with 3,074 adults (Ryskin & Brown-Schmidt 2014). No study scores predicted questions against the questions readers then asked.

**A condition retold in words was read in two ways.** This too is measured. Asked what twelve conditional sentences of requirements mean, 104 practitioners split 46.9 % to 51 % on whether the condition is also necessary, and kept disagreeing when domain knowledge was assumed (Fischbach et al. 2021). A bare "30 % chance of rain" was given its intended reference class by a majority of pedestrians in one of five cities (Gigerenzer et al. 2005), which is the first of the four things the clause asks a number to say. Against the formula: beginning algebra students solved problems stated in words more successfully than the same problems as equations (Koedinger & Nathan 2004, by its abstract); the equation stood alone there, where the clause puts it beside the sentence that says what it is for.

**One word for several things, names defined through one another, a signal named after the wrong thing.** The tests found are of identifiers in code. Developers found a semantic defect faster with full words than with abbreviations or letters, 0.78 against 0.65 and 0.66 defects a minute for 72 professionals (Hofmeister, Siegmund & Holt 2017), and about 14 % faster with descriptive compound names for 88 developers (Schankin et al. 2018); with names or comments that disagree with what the code does, 15 students showed a higher measured cognitive load and localized the bug in 75 % of tasks against over 90 % without them (Fakhoury et al. 2018). No test was found of one word for several things in a document, or of circular against non-circular definitions; a plain-language guide of the United States government asks for one term per concept.

**Removal of a part was not taken as proof.** No test compares a failing simpler scheme with the removal of a part. The nearest test bears on the readers' own wording: an explanation that contrasted the adviser's choice with the choice many people make taught more than a one-sided one, 0.47 against 0.39 for 628 crowdworkers, while a contrast with the participant's own earlier choice did not differ from the one-sided explanation (Buçinca et al. 2025). The clause therefore says "the simpler scheme the reader would build", and its hypothesis records this result.

**No simplified model for each factor.** Learners given the elements of a complex matter one by one before all together learned better in four experiments with vocational students (Pollock, Chandler & Sweller 2002), and the effect reversed for the more expert students of another study (Blayney, Kalyuga & Sweller 2010). For a quantity made of parts, a unit square split into nested parts beat a tree diagram, 0.66 against 0.38 correct on the item that turns on the nesting (Böcherer-Linder & Eichler 2017). No test of pseudocode against the same rule in sentences was found.

**A measurement without its scenario, command and spread.** The same speedup of one compiler setting ranged from below 0.95 to near 1.10 with the order in which a program was linked (Mytkowicz et al. 2009). In simulations calibrated on five tasks a comparison of single runs gave about 10 % false positives and 75 % false negatives (Bouthillier et al. 2021). Of 298 artifacts published with papers of one field, 56 % did not run at all and 20 % ran and recreated the claimed results (Olszewski et al. 2023). The documents that prescribe the practice each cover a part of the clause: all ask for the setup and for repeated runs; the run rules of one benchmark ask for the configuration file and one invocation and take the median; one checklist asks for a measure of variability; none asks for all three.

**The page was reviewed whole.** No test compares a check of a first section with a check of the finished text. Sets of five readers found between 55 % and 99 % of the problems of a tested product (Faulkner 2003), so one reader of the team is a weak instrument and the clause adds no claim that he finds all. One finding bears against the premise that a late-found fault costs much more: over 171 software projects no delayed-issue effect was found (Menzies et al. 2017). For the retelling: answers in the reader's own words scored 23.8 of 39 where true-or-false statements on the same content scored 32.4 (MacQueen et al. 2014), the nearest test of free retelling against closed questions.

**A rule in instructions against a check in the builder.** In one preprint coding agents broke a stated rule in 14 % to 50 % of runs and never under enforcement, a difference that reached significance in none of its three studies after correction (Heddad 2026). In another, a reminder left compliance nearly flat while one round of feedback from a check lifted it to 90 % and above (Yang, He & Zhou 2026). In clinical software hard stops beat soft ones in three of four comparisons (Powers et al. 2018).

## Open questions

- The same readers' reading of the rebuilt page, with the count of questions the page already answers: fewer than six is the recorded threshold.
- Readers who have not read the code, on a page built in the new order: the severe test of the order.
- A measurement of our own for the retelling: whether the check of a step-by-step retelling of a first part fails on a text with one step removed more often than answers to questions mark the removal. It ran once, `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/handing-over-to-readers/evals/retelling/runs/iteration-1`, 48 readings on Claude Opus 5.5, and went against the claim: both arms marked 12 of 12 removals, the retelling with 0 of 12 intact texts marked and the answers with 6 of 12. Each marking answer names the number of steps the opening announces, so a text that names no number is untested. The hypothesis keeps its standing after one failure.
- Leads that ended without a test: an epitome first against another sequence; an overview first against code first; predicted against real questions of readers; one term for several concepts in a document; circular against non-circular definitions; a condition apart from its detection; a failing simpler baseline against the removal of a part; pseudocode against prose; a segmented bar against a table of definitions; a measurement with scenario, command and spread against its number; early feedback on a first section against feedback on the full draft; retelling against questions for a removed step; a number reported without its period.
- Sources seen in a summary alone and not opened, among them the experiment on misleading variable names against meaningless ones, the comparison of structured flowcharts with pseudocode and the drafting directives of a standards body on one term per concept: the files of the research run list 39.
