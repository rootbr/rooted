# What the rules of handing-over-to-readers rest on — sources re-opened and evidence found to 2026-11-22

The provenance map of the skill, `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/handing-over-to-readers/maintenance/rules-provenance.md`, holds 79 lines: 2 claims stand as supported, 2 lines are norms, 1 is a definition and 74 are decisions of the repository owner, of which 73 name a hypothesis and the clause removed from the skill names its goal alone. The list of hypotheses, `maintenance/pending-evidence.md` in the same skill, holds 86 hypotheses; 81 of them name 400 tests in all, and no test among them ran on a report, a question or an explanation page that an agent wrote for the operator of a coding session or for a client. The re-opening of the cited sources found 67 places where the repository's text says other than its source: 55 in the provenance lines, where they are corrected, 2 in entries of the list of hypotheses, and 10 in the run set, where prepared edits wait for the owner's decision. A search for tests published to 2026-11-22 raised 199 proposals, and a skeptic who read each against its source let 49 stand as stated, found 150 weaker than stated and found none false.

Main results:

- Two clauses stand as supported claims with the fit "transfer", R-13 (the addressee's familiar, short word) and the length of an explanation, each on tests with lay adults, patients or students and not with the skill's readers.
- Three records that claimed support are the owner's decisions: R-12, one picture for each part, and a wrong model named before it is corrected, each because an independent reader found that it fails the rule of no fitting together with one or two further rules of the method.
- Ten places of the run set say other than their sources, four in a figure or quoted wording and six in a claim, and the run set stays unchanged until the owner decides on the prepared edits.
- Of the 49 proposals that bear for or against a line, the skeptic let 11 stand as stated, all of them against a line, and none of the 49 ran inside the scope of the line it bears on.
- 168 cited sources stayed unopened or were read through an abstract, a catalogue record or a text that quotes them, and the provenance lines say which.

## What was investigated and why

The run set of the skill is `SKILL.md` and the files under `references/`: `rules.md` with rules R-1 to R-25 and five clauses added to them, `explaining.md`, `intake.md`, `evidence.md` and `page.md`. The provenance map has one line for each clause of this run set: 26 lines for the rules (R-18 has a second line for the typed answer of an irreversible decision), 5 for the added clauses, 19 for explaining, 10 for media, 5 for the page, 5 for intake, 2 for the question gate, 1 for feedback, 4 for the explainer, 1 for `SKILL.md` and 1 for a clause the owner removed. Each line names the sources the text cites, the origin of the clause and the record of what the clause rests on, written by the scientific method of `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/scientific-method/references/method.md`.

Two questions were asked of every line. The first is whether each cited source says what the repository's text says it says, checked against the text of the source. The second is which test or document bears on the claim of the line: a comparison that could have come out against it, or the document of an issuer the clause follows.

The run answers neither of two further questions. It does not say whether a clause helps the skill's own readers, because no test found ran on the skill's deliverables. It runs no test of its own and changes nothing in the run set.

## Method

The cited sources of every line were fetched again on 2026-11-22. The words and figures quoted from them are kept with their sources in `maintenance/citations.json`, which holds 5,301 quotes, and `plugins/evidence-based-authoring/skills/scientific-method/maintenance/scripts/check_quotes.py` finds every one of them in the fetched texts.

Each of 78 lines went to one researcher, four researchers taking two lines each, and the removed clause was recorded by hand. A researcher compared each citation of the line with its source, searched for tests published to 2026-11-22 that bear on the line's claim, and wrote the line's record.

A test, in this note, is a comparison with a measure and stated conditions that could have come out against the claim. A norm is the document of an issuer the clause follows, and it tests nothing. By method.md, a rule needed before its test is in force as the owner's decision, and its claim waits as a hypothesis in the list of hypotheses, with the tests found for it, what each showed and under which conditions.

Every record that claimed support was read by an independent reader who saw nothing of the work behind it and answered the check questions of the seven rules of method.md: kind, refutability, severity, reproducibility, bounds of transfer, no fitting and revision. A record that failed a rule was amended and read again, or lowered to the owner's decision with its claim kept as a hypothesis.

A skeptic read each of the 199 proposals against its source, which the skeptic opened, and returned a verdict (stands as stated, weaker than stated, or false), the proposal's bearing on its line, and whether the test ran inside the line's scope. 71 lines went back to a writer with the skeptic's reading, and their text follows that reading.

A place where the repository's text says other than its source was corrected in the provenance line. Where the place is in the run set, an edit was prepared and left for the owner.

The run has limits. One researcher read each line, so a line's search was not repeated. The search had a budget, and a test it did not reach is not in this note. Some sources were read through an abstract or through a text that quotes them, and the provenance lines name each. The readers and the skeptic are models of one family. No test found ran on a report, a question or an explanation page that an agent wrote for the operator of a coding session or for a client.

## The records that claimed support

Five records claimed support. Two hold all seven rules of method.md and stand as supported claims; three are lowered to the owner's decisions.

R-13 asks that a term that grates on the addressee be replaced by his familiar, short word. It is a claim, supported, with the fit "transfer". Its first test is Gotlieb et al. 2022: 215 lay adults at the 2021 Minnesota State Fair understood "nothing by mouth" in 162 cases (75.3 %) against 24 (11.2 %) for "NPO", and a second pair of phrases in 208 (96.7 %) against 186 (86.5 %), both McNemar P < .001, while one reader in four did not understand the plain phrase. Its second test is Grossman Liu et al. 2022: 60 heart-failure patients, 30 an arm, understood 9.5 of 10 terms with abbreviations expanded against 6.2 (95 % CI 9.3–9.7 against 5.3–7.0, P < .001), the difference significant for HF, hx and HTN alone and not for MI (37 % against 53 %, adjusted P > .99). Bullock et al. 2019 measured self-reported fluency and not understanding (M 4.57 against 5.27, F(1,636) = 76.03, η² = .11), and the record does not count it as a test. Metzing & Brennan 2003 and Kronmüller & Barr 2015 are the origin of the clause and are counted in no test. The first independent reading found that the record failed rule 6, because Metzing & Brennan 2003 stood both as origin and as test; a second reading of the amended record found that it holds all seven rules. The tests differ from the skill in three ways: the readers were lay adults and patients reading medical phrases, not the operator or a client; the familiar wording was longer than the jargon, so the word "short" of the clause is untested; and no test reads text a model wrote.

The length clause of `references/explaining.md` asks for as many sentences as the reader needs to retell the matter, and for twenty to thirty short sentences, about two hundred words, for a small change. It is a claim, supported, with the fit "transfer". Its first test is Allwood, Wikstrom & Reder 1982: 16 undergraduates recalled more central ideas of two textbook chapters from summaries of about 1,000 and 800 words than from the chapters of 4,300 and 4,900 words (F(1,12) = 22.9, p < 0.01; 23.5 against 18.8, F(1,12) = 5.08, p < 0.05). Its second test is Mayer, Heiser & Lonn 2001: six entertaining sentences added to a narrated lesson lowered 78 students' retention from 5.07 to 3.76 (F(1,74) = 14.39, p < .001) and transfer from 3.95 to 3.05 (F(1,74) = 5.67). An independent reader found that the record holds all seven rules, and a second reading, made after three of its clauses were brought to the skeptic's reading of their sources, found the same. The tests differ from the skill: study time was fixed, the summaries ran to 800–1,000 words, and the size of twenty to thirty sentences and about two hundred words is untested. Three tests bound the claim: in Charney, Reder & Wells 1988 the cut manual was worse for readers not told their tasks; in Friedman et al. 2026 the shortest outputs were the worst at catching a wrong conclusion; in Steyvers et al. 2025 long explanations did not discriminate better than uncertainty-only responses (AUC 0.54 against 0.57, BF = .23).

R-12 asks that a fact the addressee does not know be explained in one sentence before it is relied on, and that a code, an abbreviation or an internal term be spelled out at its first mention. The independent reader found that it fails rule 6, because the half of the claim on reading time rests on its origin alone, Haviland & Clark 1974, and rule 7, because the record joins two claims under one refuting outcome. The clause is in force as the owner's decision, and its hypothesis waits.

The clause "one picture for each part, drawn in every form" was read as failing rule 4, because two sources of its ground did not open in full, and rule 6, because the meta-analyses it cites, Guo et al. 2020 among them, are also the origin of the clause. Its later tests do not separate the picture: Schneider et al. 2024 gave the diagram together with traceability information (analysis correctness 6.75 against 4.79, p = 0.0025), and Hao et al. 2026 is an observational comparison. The clause is in force as the owner's decision, and its hypothesis waits.

The clause "a wrong model is named before it is corrected" was read as failing rule 3, because no test found varies the order of the wrong model and its correction in the form the clause prescribes, rule 6, because Schroeder & Kucera 2022 is its origin, and rule 7, because Swire-Thompson et al. 2021, Exp. 3 and 4 (p = .13, BF01 = 2.26; p = .625, BF01 = 8.01), meet the outcome the record names as refuting. By the table of standings of method.md that outcome makes the claim contested. The clause is in force as the owner's decision, and its hypothesis waits.

## Tests for and against a claim

33 proposals bear against a line and 16 bear in a line's direction. The skeptic let 11 stand as stated, all of them against a line, and found the other 38 weaker than stated. None of the 49 ran inside the scope of its line, so none of them is support and none refutes a line.

R-19 orders a question's facts first and the question last. Metzler, Kunz & Fuchs 2015 placed a definition before or after the question stem in web surveys of applicants to Darmstadt University of Technology (n = 5,977, 7,395 and 5,996), and the definition after the stem changed answers more in all four studies, Bonferroni-corrected at .05. The conditions differ: university applicants answered open numeric survey questions, not an operator answering an agent's item. The skeptic found the figures as the source gives them and named what the test lacks: its measure is a shift in self-reported answers, and it concerns one kind of supporting text, a definition.

The media line on text and page with voice sets text alone against text with a page and voice. Rogowsky, Calhoun & Tallal 2016 gave 91 participants the preface and a chapter of a non-fiction book as an audiobook, an e-text or both at once, and comprehension showed no statistically significant difference; the abstract names no test. The conditions differ: a chapter without figures, a voice laid over the same text, and general readers. The skeptic found that it bears against the voice component alone, and that the power of the null result cannot be judged from the abstract.

The media line on lighting what is spoken claims that a lit fragment helps the listening reader. Gerbier, Bailly & Bosse 2018 lit words as they were spoken for 40 children of grades 3 to 5 reading while listening, and the effect was null for the spelling of pseudowords and negative for their meaning; the abstract gives no figure. The conditions differ: children, stories, single words lit and unlit words greyed out.
