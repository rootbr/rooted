# Provenance of the method

One line per part of `references/method.md`, keyed `M-n`, and one per prescription of `SKILL.md` that the method does not carry, keyed `S-n`: the texts or the rules its formulation follows, then its record. The parts of the method are conventions the repository adopts, so their records are decisions and definitions; the texts below are the formulations those decisions follow. What would count as a test of the method stands in `maintenance/pending-evidence.md`.

How each text was seen: `first-hand` marks a text opened with a fetch tool that returns extracted passages, the quoted words being as that tool returned them; Mayo's §1.1 was read in the publisher's excerpt as extracted text; the selections from *The Logic of Scientific Discovery* were read in a course document whose text carries scanning errors. `SEP` is the Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy, https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/<slug>/. A thinker named with an SEP section is reported as that entry reports him; his own book was opened only where a line says so.

- **M-0** · the opening of the method: a claim is judged by its test, and the rules are a convention applied alike · SEP scientific-method §6.1 ("the legend of a single, universal scientific method"), §7 ("scientific knowledge differs from other kinds of knowledge, especially everyday knowledge, primarily by being more systematic"), first-hand; SEP pseudo-science §8 ("almost complete disagreement on the general criteria", beside "virtual unanimity" on particular cases), first-hand; Popper, *The Logic of Scientific Discovery*, selections at https://earthweb.ess.washington.edu/roe/Knowability_590/Week1/Popper.doc ("Methodological rules are here regarded as conventions"; "They might be described as the rules of the game of empirical science"), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: one stated way of admitting rules, applied to every statement alike · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-1** · facts apart from decisions · SEP hume-moral §5 (the dominant reading of Hume's passage: "no ethical or indeed evaluative conclusion whatsoever may be validly inferred from any set of purely factual premises"; the entry reports other readings), first-hand; SEP pseudo-science §4.2 (Popper's demarcation separates empirical science from mathematics, logic and metaphysics), first-hand; SEP popper §2 ("unscientific theories may be enlightening"), first-hand; SEP scientific-objectivity §3.3 (values enter in deciding "what should count as a sufficient reason" and not as reasons in themselves), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: a taste, a convention or a norm never enters the rules presented as a finding · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-2** · refutability · SEP pseudo-science §4.3 (a scientific statement is "capable of conflicting with possible, or conceivable observations"), first-hand; SEP popper §3 ("Every genuine scientific theory then, in Popper's view, is prohibitive"), first-hand; SEP operationalism §2.1 (Bridgman: "we mean by any concept nothing more than a set of operations", with his later withdrawal of the synonymy) and §3.2, first-hand; National Academy of Sciences, *Teaching About Evolution and the Nature of Science* (1998), as reproduced at https://physics.weber.edu/carroll/honors/scidefs.htm ("A tentative statement about the natural world leading to deductions that can be tested"), first-hand for the reproduction · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: a claim that no outcome could count against stays out of the rules · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-3** · severity of the test · SEP popper §3 (corroboration counts "only if it is the positive result of a genuinely 'risky' prediction, which might conceivably have been false"), first-hand; Mayo, *Statistical Inference as Severe Testing* (Cambridge University Press, 2018) §1.1, excerpt at https://assets.cambridge.org/97811076/64647/excerpt/9781107664647_excerpt.pdf ("One does not have evidence for a claim if nothing has been done to rule out ways the claim may be false."), first-hand; SEP scientific-method §4 (Neyman and Pearson: "the consequence of error also had to play a role when deciding between hypotheses"), first-hand; SEP physics-experiment §2.1 (calibration, independent confirmation and the elimination of alternative explanations "do not, however, guarantee that the results are correct"), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: agreement that would have appeared without the technique supports nothing · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-4** · reproducibility · Popper, selections as under M-0 ("non-reproducible single occurrences are of no significance to science"; "only such statements may be introduced into science as are intersubjectively testable"), first-hand; SEP scientific-objectivity §5.2 (Popper: "the objectivity of scientific statements lies in the fact that they can be inter-subjectively tested") and §4.1 (Porter: "Objectivity means knowledge that does not depend too much on the particular individuals who author it"), first-hand; the Royal Society on its motto, https://royalsociety.org/about-us/who-we-are/history/ ("take nobody's word for it"; "withstand the domination of authority"), first-hand; SEP scientific-knowledge-social §2 (Hardwig: in large teams "all must take one anothers' results on trust"), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: a rule rests on a test another person can repeat or open, and never on who said it · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-5** · bounds of transfer · SEP induction-problem §1–2 (the inference from observed to unobserved cases presupposes a uniformity that reason does not establish: "it implies no contradiction that the course of nature may change"), first-hand; Deaton and Cartwright, "Understanding and misunderstanding randomized controlled trials", doi:10.1016/j.socscimed.2017.12.005, read at https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6019115/ ("the truth we are referring to is the truth in the trial sample only"; "justification is required to extend the results to other groups"), first-hand for the abstract and §1; SEP operationalism §3.2 ("not to make conceptual extensions without operational grounds"), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: a result is not stretched past the conditions it was obtained under without saying so · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-6** · no fitting · SEP popper §2 and §12 (a theory saved "by the addition of ad hoc hypotheses which made it compatible with the facts"), first-hand; Popper, selections as under M-0 (the rules "must be designed in such a way that they do not protect any statement in science against falsification"), first-hand; SEP lakatos §2.2 (auxiliary hypotheses "all cooked up after the event to protect Marxian theory from the facts"; a progressive programme predicts novel facts), first-hand; SEP scientific-reproducibility §2.3 (hypothesising after the results are known: "presenting ad hoc and/or unexpected findings as though they had been predicted all along", after Kerr 1998) and §4.2 (preregistration serves to "distinguish prediction from postdiction"), first-hand; Mayo §1.1 as under M-3, quoting Fisher 1955 ("presenting only a selected subset of the data available"), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: a prediction stands in history before its result, and a refuted claim is not kept alive by a patch · hypothesis: H-01
- **M-7** · revision · SEP popper §5 ("all knowledge is provisional, conjectural, hypothetical"; a tested theory is "corroborated (but never verified)"), first-hand; Popper, selections as under M-0 ("we shall take it as falsified only if we discover a reproducible effect which refutes the theory"), first-hand; SEP scientific-underdetermination §2.1 (Duhem: an experiment condemns a group of hypotheses and "does not designate which one should be changed"), first-hand; SEP lakatos §2.2 ("all theories, in this sense, are born refuted and die refuted"), first-hand; Hume, *An Enquiry concerning Human Understanding* 10.4, https://davidhume.org/texts/e/10 ("A wise man, therefore, proportions his belief to the evidence."), first-hand · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: no rule is held as proved, and a test that went against a rule stays on record · hypothesis: H-01

- **S-3** · a claim about how a program, a language or a tool behaves takes a test of one run · follows from M-3: repetitions tell an effect from chance, and a behaviour that is the same on every run leaves no chance to tell it from · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: no repetitions are spent where one run decides
- **S-4** · a test in which an agent follows an instruction bounds only the claim that the instruction produces the behaviour · follows from M-2 and M-5: the measure of such a test is the behaviour, so the benefit of the behaviour lies outside its bounds · kind: decision · owner: the repository owner · goal: a test of compliance is never presented as a test of benefit
