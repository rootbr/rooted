# Research note — Architecture decisions and their record

Topic `architecture-decisions-and-their-record` of group K. Architecture, domain `architecture`, id prefix `ARCH`. Formulation locators: SWEBOK Guide V4.0 ch. 2 'Software Architecture' (architecture decisions, rationale); ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 (architecture rationale, correspondence); Nygard, 'Documenting architecture decisions' (2011, formulation of the ADR); Software Engineering at Google ch. 10 'Documentation' (design documents).

The canon agrees on what a decision record holds and when to write one. The Nygard format (status, context, decision, consequences) is the template most open-source logs use, per a relayed MSR study of 921 repositories. A decision that affects the architecture is recorded in the repository of the application it affects, per the GDS Way (fetched). SWEBOK V4 ch. 2 §2.4 requires each nontrivial decision to be documented with its rationale; I opened it only in a community Russian translation. Each record holds one decision (MADR, adr.github.io, Henderson). Consequences list the bad as well as the good (GDS Way, the adr-tools template, MADR's Good/Bad list, Nygard as relayed). A reversal is a new record that supersedes the old one: every camp agrees, the mutability camp included. Two points are disputed, and neither blocks this topic's cards. On scope, Nygard and arc42 record only architecturally significant decisions, while MADR records any important decision; both camps count adding, removing or replacing a dependency, framework or vendor, so the trigger rule sits inside both. On immutability, Nygard, PEP 1, the Rust RFC process, adr-tools and log4brains keep an accepted record fixed, while Henderson reports that dated living documents worked better and the GDS Way allows edits until the decision is implemented. The documentation domain already settled that dispute as DOC-16, and DOC-15 already requires the rationale, so neither is re-carded here. This topic carries what lies between the code change and the record. A change that makes a component-level decision adds a record where the project keeps a log. A change that reverses an accepted record's decision, once the decision was implemented, adds a superseding record. An accepted record brings the architecture documentation along. A dependency ban encoded in an analyzer states why it exists. Record hygiene adds three rules: an explicit scope, one decision per record, and unique numbering. No source measures an outcome of recording a dependency or framework change, or of immutable over living records. The cards therefore rest on official documentation (the GDS Way, MADR, adr-tools, the Go, Rust and Python processes), on tool documentation (cargo-deny, Ruff TID251, ESLint, gomodguard, depguard, dependency-cruiser) and on relayed research (Jansen and Bosch on decisions as additions and subtractions to the architecture; Buchgeher et al. 2023 on low but growing adoption; an ICSA 2026 study finding that about 63% of 5,800 ADRs were opened directly as accepted). Three rules stay pending. The record's cost of change and its compliance check have formulation only. Recording rejected alternatives remains contested, as the documentation domain found, although SWEBOK V4 §2.4 now adds a standard-class source to the side that records them.

## Formulation layer

The canon agrees on what a decision record states: one decision per record, written down with its context, the options considered, the rationale and every consequence, good and bad. Nygard (relayed), Tyree/Akerman, MADR, PEP 1, the Rust RFC template, the Go proposal template, the KEP template and SE at Google ch. 10 all say this, and every one of those except Nygard was fetched. Two points are contested. On scope, Nygard and arc42 limit records to "architecturally significant" decisions, those that affect structure, quality characteristics, dependencies, interfaces or construction techniques, while MADR records "any (important) decision". On immutability, Nygard, PEP 1, the Rust RFC process and adr-tools keep an accepted record fixed and supersede it with a new one, while Henderson's README reports that "mutability has worked better" and the GDS Way allows clarifying edits until part of the decision has been implemented. The trigger rule ("a change of this kind carries a record") appears as a process requirement in the Rust RFC, Go proposal and Google design-doc processes and as a CI gate (ADR Guard, fetched); no canon source names "cost of change" as a required consequence field, and the nearest are Nygard's "what becomes easier or more difficult" and the GDS note on choices that "can therefore be changed with little impact". The SWEBOK V4 ch. 2 §2.4 body text and the ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 normative text could not be opened: only their clause locations and secondary summaries were relayed, so what they say is given as relayed snippets or as a paraphrase marked unfetched. The six-search budget was spent before Tang et al. 2006, Capilla et al. 2016 and Zimmermann et al. 2015 could be searched; they are listed as unfetched and back no card.

## Reception layer

Practitioners agree on the core: an ADR holds the context, the decision and its consequences, it lives in the repository rather than a wiki, and a reversed decision gets a new record that supersedes the old one. Nygard, Fowler (relayed), log4brains, adr-tools, PEP 1 and the Rust RFC process all say this, and even the mutability camp keeps it. Two points are contested. On immutability, Joel Parker Henderson's ADR collection says "in practice, mutability has worked better" and recommends dated addenda, and a javacodegeeks critique (relayed) argues that immutable records drift away from the system without any signal, against log4brains' claim that they are "never out-of-date". On scope, MADR (Kopp, Zimmermann) extends records to "any decision" beyond the architecturally significant ones, and MADR also makes Consequences an optional section. Mechanical checking comes in two kinds. ADR Guard and Decision Guardian check that a record exists or surface it on a PR touching watched paths, and ADR Guard ignores lockfiles on purpose because "a gate that fires on a dependency bump gets switched off within a fortnight". ArchUnit, Nx, dependency-cruiser, deptrac, depguard and cargo-deny check the decision itself on component dependencies, and cargo-deny and depguard attach the reason or the replacement to the ban. My searches turned up no Hacker News or Lobsters threads; the papers Tyree and Akerman, Jansen and Bosch, and Capilla et al. were left to the evidence layer, and the Buchgeher 2023 and ICSA 2026 adoption figures are relayed from search summaries only.

## Evidence layer

The ADR canon is well documented in openable GitHub form: the adr-tools template and its supersede command, the MADR template, the Tyree-Akerman template as reproduced by joelparkerhenderson, the UK GDS Way standard page, log4brains, the adr.github.io definitions and the ADR Guard CI action were all fetched with curl. The research side was reachable only through six WebSearch summaries. Buchgeher et al. 2023 (IEEE Access) measured low but growing ADR adoption on GitHub, found that about 50% of repositories with ADRs hold only one to five records, and found Nygard's template dominant. An ICSA 2026 follow-up reports that about 63% of 5,800 ADRs were opened directly as accepted. Tang et al., Weinreich et al. 2015, Weinreich and Groher 2016, Jansen and Bosch 2005 and ISO/IEC/IEEE 42010:2022 clause 5.2.12 were relayed only in part, and the abstracts of Capilla 2016, Jansen-Bosch and Zimmermann 2015 could not be opened. No source found measures an outcome effect of recording a component-dependency or framework change, or of immutability over living documents, so those rules rest on standards, tool documentation and formulation rather than measured evidence. The canon disagrees on two points: immutability (Nygard, adr-tools, log4brains and Arachne versus joelparkerhenderson's 'mutability has worked better' and the GDS Way's conditional allowance for clarifying edits before implementation) and whether Consequences is mandatory (Nygard and adr-tools make it a section, while MADR marks it optional).
